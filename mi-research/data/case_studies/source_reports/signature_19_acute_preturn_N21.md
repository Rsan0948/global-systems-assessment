# The Acute Pre-Turn Configuration: 19 Test Cases to Move the Modernization Index Signature from N=2 to N=21

## TL;DR
- The four-component signature (P1 declining from a high base + P5 declining + pillar spread widening + P4 holding strong) holds up as a **moderately strong but imperfect predictor**: across 19 new cases it correctly flags most surprise institutional crises in consolidated states, but it produces real false positives (Poland, Hungary, France) where the deterioration was absorbed, and it misses crises where the economy was *also* failing (Argentina 2001, Venezuela, Egypt) or where collapse came with little prior erosion (South Korea 2024).
- Lead times cluster at **3–6 years**, consistent with Chile (~5) and Lebanon (~3); the signature's distinguishing feature — a strong economy masking institutional/stability erosion — is genuinely diagnostic, but the difference between an "absorber" and a "non-absorber" appears to lie in **counter-pressures the MI does not directly measure**: independent courts, federalism, EU/external anchoring, civil-society mobilization capacity, and electoral integrity.
- For the **US and UK flags**: the signature is real and the underlying WGI data confirm a genuine decline from a high base with the economy holding — but the existence of Poland-type absorbers means the flag should be read as **elevated risk, not destiny**. The US is the more concerning of the two because its Political Stability score has fallen further and faster.

## Key Findings
- **Strongest confirming (true-positive) cases:** Turkey (2007–2013), Thailand (2008–2014), Brazil (2010–2016), South Africa (2009–2021), Peru (2016–2022). All were considered consolidated or "success stories," all show institutional + stability erosion under a strong or steady economy, and all produced surprise acute crises within the 3–7 year window.
- **Most important disconfirming cases (false positives):** Poland (2015–2023) and Hungary (2010–2023) both displayed the configuration — high-base institutional decline, stability/cohesion erosion, widening imbalance, robust GDP growth — yet neither produced an acute rupture; Poland actually reversed course via the 2023 election. France (2014–2018) is a milder false positive.
- **False negatives that bound the signature's sensitivity:** South Korea's December 2024 martial-law crisis erupted from a high, stable institutional base with little prior erosion; Argentina 2001, Venezuela (Caracazo era) and Egypt 2011 all had crises where the economy was collapsing or institutions were already weak — violating the "economy masks the rot" premise.
- **Clean true negatives:** Canada, Germany, Japan and Uruguay faced major external stress (2008 crisis, COVID, regional democratic recession) without the configuration and without acute crisis — though Germany's Political Stability sub-score has quietly drifted down.

## Details

### CATEGORY 1 — TRUE POSITIVES

---

**CASE 1 — TURKEY (2007–2013 window; 2013–2016 outcome)**
- **Category:** True positive
- **P1 trajectory:** Declining from a moderately high, EU-accession-era base. Control of Corruption peaked at +0.16 in 2012 then fell; Rule of Law has since collapsed to roughly -0.84 by 2024. The inflection is the December 2013 corruption probe that Erdoğan derailed. P1 was clearly eroding 2011–2013.
- **P5 trajectory:** Declining — Gezi Park protests (mid-2013) signaled cohesion/legitimacy strain; political stability worsened sharply through the 2016 coup attempt.
- **Spread:** Widening — economic pillar stayed strong while institutional/voice measures fell, opening a large gap.
- **P4 status:** Strong. Turkey was a celebrated emerging-market growth story through 2013 (post-2009 rebound, large GDP gains), masking the institutional capture underneath.
- **Outcome:** Gezi protests (2013), derailed corruption investigation (Dec 2013), July 2016 coup attempt and mass purges (4,000+ judges/prosecutors removed). Lead time ~ a couple of years to first acute event, longer to authoritarian consolidation.
- **Why this case:** Classic "economy looks fine, institutions captured" pattern; a textbook fit.
- **Surprise?** Partly — Turkey was widely held up as a Muslim-democracy success model pre-2013.
- **Lead time:** ~2–3 years to Gezi/corruption probe; ~3 years to the coup attempt.
- **Data availability:** FULL.
- **Confidence:** HIGH.

---

**CASE 2 — THAILAND (2008–2014 window; 2014 outcome)**
- **Category:** True positive
- **P1 trajectory:** Declining from a relatively high base for the region; judicial independence and rule of law politicized through repeated court interventions (annulled 2014 election).
- **P5 trajectory:** Sharply declining — color-coded street conflict, the PDRC occupation of Bangkok, 28 deaths and 800+ injuries during 2013–14 protests, state of emergency Jan 2014.
- **Spread:** Widening — economy resilient while political-stability pillar cratered.
- **P4 status:** Strong/steady. Thaksinomics had more than doubled rural (Isan) GDP per capita 2001–2011 to ~US$1,475; a synthetic-control study (Suwanprasert, 2024) found the 2014 coup had no statistically significant effect on GDP — i.e., the economy was not the problem.
- **Outcome:** 22 May 2014 military coup; junta (NCPO); partial repeal of constitution.
- **Why this case:** Strong economy with collapsing political stability — directly tests the "economy masks instability" claim.
- **Surprise?** Moderately — coups in Thailand are recurrent, lowering the surprise factor.
- **Lead time:** ~5–6 years of rising imbalance from the 2008–09 turbulence to 2014.
- **Data availability:** FULL.
- **Confidence:** HIGH (with caveat that Thailand's coup-proneness reduces the "stable/consolidated" precondition).

---

**CASE 3 — BRAZIL (2010–2016 window; 2013–2016 outcome)**
- **Category:** True positive
- **P1 trajectory:** Declining from a high regional base as the Petrobras/Lava Jato corruption nexus surfaced; control of corruption and rule of law eroded.
- **P5 trajectory:** Declining — the June 2013 mass protests peaked on the night of 20 June 2013 with an estimated 1.2 million Brazilians in more than 100 cities (cumulatively over 2 million participants), the largest demonstrations since the 1992 protests against President Collor de Mello.
- **Spread:** Widening through the commodity-boom plateau (boom ended ~2012).
- **P4 status:** Strong at the front of the window — Brazil was a BRIC darling; New Year 2013 polling (Folha de S.Paulo) showed 92% expecting their economic situation to improve or stay stable. (Caveat: the economy weakened markedly into the 2015–16 recession, so P4 "masked" only in the early window.)
- **Outcome:** June 2013 protests; 2016 impeachment of Dilma Rousseff; prolonged legitimacy crisis culminating in Bolsonaro (2018).
- **Why this case:** The 2013 protests' "explosive growth, size and extraordinary reach caught everyone – the left, the right, and the government – by surprise" (Saad-Filho); Americas Quarterly: "Virtually no one saw the demonstrations coming."
- **Surprise?** Yes — unambiguously unanticipated by all sides.
- **Lead time:** ~months from late-stage signal to 2013 protests; ~3 years to impeachment.
- **Data availability:** FULL.
- **Confidence:** MODERATE-HIGH (P4 deteriorated mid-window).

---

**CASE 4 — SOUTH AFRICA (2009–2021 window; 2021 outcome)**
- **Category:** True positive
- **P1 trajectory:** Declining from a high post-apartheid base through "state capture" under Zuma (2009–2018) — government effectiveness, control of corruption and rule of law all eroded; State of Capture report (2016), Zondo Commission (2018).
- **P5 trajectory:** Declining — culminating in the July 2021 riots/unrest.
- **Spread:** Widening — strong financial/economic institutions vs. collapsing governance.
- **P4 status:** Steady-to-weak — "sclerotic growth since 2008," so P4 was *not* robustly strong. This is a partial fit: the economy stagnated rather than boomed.
- **Outcome:** July 2021 unrest (the worst violence since apartheid) following Zuma's arrest; sustained institutional damage (Eskom, Transnet).
- **Why this case:** Tests the signature where institutions eroded badly but the economy merely stagnated — a boundary case.
- **Surprise?** The July 2021 violence's scale surprised many.
- **Lead time:** ~7–9 years of capture before the 2021 acute episode.
- **Data availability:** GOOD.
- **Confidence:** MODERATE (P4 stagnant, not strong).

---

**CASE 5 — PERU (2016–2022 window; 2022 outcome)**
- **Category:** True positive (best-fit "economy masks the crisis" case)
- **P1 trajectory:** Declining — congressional weaponization of impeachment, judiciary and prosecutor capture, "pro-crime laws"; eight presidents in a decade.
- **P5 trajectory:** Declining — escalating executive-legislative conflict, Castillo's December 2022 self-coup attempt, deadly protests, states of emergency.
- **Spread:** Widening dramatically — politics in freefall while economy held.
- **P4 status:** Strong/insulated. An independent central bank, low public debt, large reserves, commodity tailwinds kept Peru "a low-drama investment environment despite political churn." Peru's CPI averaged ~3.5% over the ten years to 2024 (FocusEconomics) versus a Latin America regional average of ~10.8% — and 2012 inflation of 1.8% was the lowest in the region.
- **Outcome:** Castillo self-coup and removal (Dec 2022); mass unrest — at least 60 deaths and 600+ injuries per Peru's Ombudsman's Office (Defensoría del Pueblo), including 17 killed in a single day in Juliaca (Jan 2023); ongoing instability through Boluarte's 2025 ouster.
- **Why this case:** The literature explicitly describes a "decoupling between politics and the economy" — the purest illustration of the economy masking institutional decay.
- **Surprise?** The depth and persistence surprised observers.
- **Lead time:** ~6 years from the 2016 inflection to the 2022 acute rupture.
- **Data availability:** GOOD.
- **Confidence:** HIGH.

---

**CASE 6 — TUNISIA (2014–2021 window; 2021 outcome)**
- **Category:** True positive (with strong caveat)
- **P1 trajectory:** Declining from the post-2011 democratic high — failure to seat a constitutional court, institutional gridlock, anti-corruption authority later dissolved.
- **P5 trajectory:** Declining — disillusionment, protests, COVID health-system collapse.
- **Spread:** Widening (institutional/legitimacy collapse vs. comparatively steady macro indicators early on).
- **P4 status:** **WEAK** — Tunisia's economy was sluggish/deteriorating well before 2021 (Moody's cut to C in 2021; current-account deficit ~7% of GDP). This violates the "strong economy masking" premise.
- **Outcome:** 25 July 2021 self-coup by President Saied (parliament suspended, constitution abandoned, 2022 referendum constitution).
- **Why this case:** A boundary case — institutional + stability erosion clearly present, but the economy was *also* failing, so it does not cleanly fit the signature.
- **Surprise?** The speed surprised; the fragility did not.
- **Lead time:** ~7 years.
- **Data availability:** GOOD.
- **Confidence:** LOW-MODERATE as a true positive; honestly, **closer to a false negative** because P4 was not strong.

---

**CASE 7 — SRI LANKA (2015–2022 window; 2022 outcome)**
- **Category:** True positive (boundary — overlaps with the framework's candidate list)
- **P1 trajectory:** Declining — governance and transparency erosion, militarized administration, abrupt policy-making (overnight fertilizer ban).
- **P5 trajectory:** Declining — culminating in the Aragalaya protests and President Rajapaksa fleeing (July 2022).
- **Spread:** Widening.
- **P4 status:** **Mixed/masking-then-collapse.** Real GDP stagnated around 3.6% annually in the decade before 2022 and the fiscal position deteriorated steadily (budget deficit averaging 7% over the decade, public debt growing ~13%/year), but headline growth and a defended exchange rate kept up appearances until the 2022 default. This is the Lebanon-style "looked solvent until it wasn't" pattern.
- **Outcome:** April 2022 sovereign default (the first in the Indo-Pacific in over two decades); inflation >70%; GDP contraction of 7.3% in 2022; mass protests; government collapse.
- **Why this case:** Closely mirrors the Lebanon confirmed case (debt-masked fragility).
- **Surprise?** The collapse's speed surprised, though debt analysts had warned.
- **Lead time:** ~3–5 years.
- **Data availability:** GOOD.
- **Confidence:** MODERATE (P4 was masking deterioration rather than genuinely strong).

---

**CASE 8 — ITALY (1989–1992 window; 1992–1994 outcome)**
- **Category:** True positive (with caveat)
- **P1 trajectory:** Declining — systemic corruption (Tangentopoli) exposed by the Mani Pulite investigation; institutions delegitimized as more than half of Parliament fell under indictment and 400+ councils were dissolved.
- **P5 trajectory:** Declining — mafia assassinations of judges Falcone and Borsellino (1992), collapse of public trust.
- **Spread:** Widening between a still-functioning economy and collapsing political legitimacy.
- **P4 status:** **Weak/deteriorating** — accompanied by a financial crisis and public debt surpassing annual GNP; the Mani Pulite campaign "intensified due to a financial crisis." P4 was not strong.
- **Outcome:** Collapse of the First Republic (1992–94); disappearance of the DC and PSI; the technocratic Ciampi government.
- **Why this case:** Pre-1996 historical depth; but the economic dimension was failing, so it's a partial fit.
- **Surprise?** The scale of collapse was unexpected.
- **Lead time:** ~3 years.
- **Data availability:** LIMITED (pre-WGI; relies on Polity/historical sources).
- **Confidence:** LOW-MODERATE (P4 weak — arguably a false negative).

### CATEGORY 2 — TRUE NEGATIVES

---

**CASE 9 — CANADA (2008–2024)**
- **Category:** True negative
- **P1:** Stable/high — WGI rule of law and government effectiveness held near the top of the global distribution throughout; political stability index ~82/100 (2023, World Bank percentile).
- **P5:** Stable — no acute institutional crisis.
- **Spread:** Stable/narrow.
- **P4:** Strong/resilient — faster post-COVID GDP and employment recovery than after 2008–09; unemployment 6.8% in late 2024, below the 8.7% post-financial-crisis peak.
- **Stress faced:** 2008 financial crisis, COVID, 2022 "Freedom Convoy."
- **Outcome:** No acute institutional crisis — genuine resilience.
- **Why this case:** Faced real stress without the configuration → true negative.
- **Data availability:** FULL. **Confidence:** HIGH.

---

**CASE 10 — GERMANY (2008–2024)**
- **Category:** True negative (with one caveat sub-score)
- **P1:** Stable/high — Rule of Law held in a tight 1.58–1.74 band 1996–2024 (1.63 in 2024); Government Effectiveness ~1.55; Control of Corruption ~1.77 in 2024. Essentially flat and elite.
- **P5:** **Caveat — declining sub-score.** Political Stability fell from +1.35 (2000) to +0.12 (2024, an all-time low), though it remained above the world average (-0.07) and never collapsed.
- **Spread:** Stable on the institutional measures.
- **P4:** Strong through 2018; weaker (near-stagnation) 2023–24.
- **Outcome:** No acute institutional crisis despite AfD rise, energy shock, COVID.
- **Why this case:** Mostly confirms a true negative; the Political-Stability drift is a useful warning that even strong states can show P5 erosion without (so far) acute rupture.
- **Data availability:** FULL. **Confidence:** MODERATE-HIGH.

---

**CASE 11 — JAPAN (2008–2024)**
- **Category:** True negative (cleanest)
- **P1:** Stable/rising — Rule of Law rose modestly to a 2021 peak of 1.53 and stood at 1.51 in 2024.
- **P5:** Stable/high — Political Stability held in a 0.97–1.36 band across 1996–2024 (1.13 in 2024, the highest in the G7).
- **Spread:** Stable.
- **P4:** Steady (low-growth but stable).
- **Outcome:** No acute crisis through the 2011 triple disaster, COVID, and Abe's assassination (2022).
- **Why this case:** Decades of high, flat governance with major shocks absorbed → textbook true negative.
- **Data availability:** FULL. **Confidence:** HIGH.

---

**CASE 12 — URUGUAY (2008–2024)**
- **Category:** True negative
- **P1:** Stable/high — South America's only "full democracy" (EIU 8.66–8.7); BTI ranks it 1st for political transformation in its set; 157th on the Fragile States Index (i.e., very resilient).
- **P5:** Stable — strong democratic tradition, low polarization.
- **Spread:** Stable/narrow.
- **P4:** Resilient — diversified exports, sound macro policy, ~3.2% growth (2024).
- **Stress faced:** COVID, regional democratic recession (the EIU's sharpest regional decline), rising crime.
- **Outcome:** No acute institutional crisis.
- **Why this case:** A non-rich-world, regionally-stressed true negative — geographic diversity for the negative class.
- **Data availability:** GOOD. **Confidence:** HIGH.

### CATEGORY 3 — FALSE POSITIVES (most important) AND FALSE NEGATIVES

---

**CASE 13 — POLAND (2015–2023) — FALSE POSITIVE (the single most important disconfirming case)**
- **Category:** False positive
- **P1 trajectory:** Declining from a high EU-accession base — V-Dem downgraded Poland to "electoral democracy" in 2016; the most democratic backsliding of any European country in the decade to 2023; capture of the Constitutional Tribunal, judiciary, prosecution and public media; Article 7 proceedings (2018).
- **P5 trajectory:** Declining — top-down polarization, mass women's-rights protests, EU rule-of-law conflict.
- **Spread:** Widening — institutional quality fell sharply while the economy thrived.
- **P4 status:** **Strong** — "consistent strong per capita economic growth since 1989," rising wages, moderate inequality. The economy unambiguously masked the institutional erosion.
- **Outcome:** **NO acute crisis.** The configuration was fully present, yet Poland absorbed it and *reversed* via the 15 October 2023 election — turnout of 74.4%, the highest in any contested election since the fall of communism (ABC News noted it was the highest since 1919 and higher than the 1989 first free elections), restoring a pro-democratic coalition.
- **Why this is critical:** All four components present + strong economy → the signature would have fired — but the outcome was democratic recovery, not rupture. **The absorber factors:** EU anchoring and conditional funds — the Commission suspended roughly €136 billion (around 17% of Poland's GDP, per the Centre for European Reform), with von der Leyen announcing the unfreezing of "up to €137 billion" in Warsaw on 23 February 2024 — plus intact electoral integrity, courts that "did not collapse entirely," and an exceptionally mobilized civil society.
- **Lead time / detectability:** Signature detectable from ~2016; no acute event in 7+ years.
- **Data availability:** FULL. **Confidence:** HIGH that this is a false positive.

---

**CASE 14 — HUNGARY (2010–2023) — FALSE POSITIVE**
- **Category:** False positive (slow autocratization, not acute crisis)
- **P1 trajectory:** Declining from a high base — between 2010 and 2024 Hungary lost ground on all WGI measures except (barely) government effectiveness; rule of law, voice, political stability, regulatory quality and control of corruption all fell; Bertelsmann political-transformation score fell from 9.35 (pre-2010) to 6.3 ("defective democracy").
- **P5 trajectory:** Declining — captured media, polarization.
- **Spread:** Widening.
- **P4 status:** Steady — Hungary kept growing (though it lagged regional peers); the economy did not collapse during the backsliding.
- **Outcome:** **NO acute rupture** — a gradual, legalistic slide to "electoral autocracy," not a coup/explosion/state of emergency. The crisis was *chronic*, not *acute*.
- **Why this is critical:** The signature does not distinguish slow autocratization (absorbed without an acute event) from imminent acute rupture. Hungary shows the configuration can resolve as a slow grind. (Note the contrast with Poland: the EU's conditional funds to Hungary, ~€32bn/~16% of GDP, did not produce a reversal — absorber factors are necessary but not sufficient.)
- **Lead time:** N/A — no discrete acute event.
- **Data availability:** FULL. **Confidence:** HIGH as a false positive (for "acute crisis").

---

**CASE 15 — FRANCE (2014–2018) — FALSE POSITIVE (mild)**
- **Category:** False positive
- **P1 trajectory:** Mild decline — perceptions of representation/legitimacy erosion; repeated use of constitutional Article 49.3 to force measures; weakening of mediation bodies.
- **P5 trajectory:** Declining — the gilets jaunes erupted in November 2018 (an online petition drew nearly 1 million signatures), the largest sustained unrest in decades, with widespread perception of "corrupted democratic representation."
- **Spread:** Modestly widening.
- **P4 status:** Steady — a large, advanced economy; Macron offered a package of economic and social concessions without fiscal crisis.
- **Outcome:** **NO acute institutional crisis** — the Fifth Republic absorbed the shock; Macron made concessions and the movement faded by mid-2020.
- **Why this case:** Demonstrates that mass unrest + mild institutional strain + steady economy can be absorbed by robust constitutional structures.
- **Data availability:** GOOD. **Confidence:** MODERATE-HIGH.

---

**CASE 16 — SOUTH KOREA (2024) — FALSE NEGATIVE**
- **Category:** False negative (acute crisis WITHOUT the prior signature)
- **P1 trajectory:** **Stable/high before the event** — fully consolidated democracy, strong rule of law, independent courts; no measurable multi-year institutional erosion of the Chile/Turkey type.
- **P5 trajectory:** Politically polarized (executive-legislative deadlock after the April 2024 opposition landslide) but not a multi-year stability collapse.
- **Spread:** Not notably widening on governance measures.
- **P4 status:** Slowing but functional — 2023 growth 1.4% (weak), Q3 2024 GDP +0.1%.
- **Outcome:** **Acute crisis** — President Yoon declared martial law at 10:30 p.m. on 3 December 2024 (the first since 1980); 190 of 300 lawmakers voted unanimously to lift it at ~01:02 on 4 December and the cabinet formally lifted it ~02:30–04:30, after which Yoon was impeached, arrested and removed (Constitutional Court, April 2025).
- **Why this is critical:** A genuine acute institutional crisis erupted with **little of the slow-erosion signature beforehand** — an idiosyncratic elite gamble, and democratic guardrails (Assembly, courts, civil society) defeated it within hours. The signature would have *missed* this; equally, the crisis was contained, underscoring that acute events ≠ collapse.
- **Lead time:** Essentially none — not detectable via slow MI erosion.
- **Data availability:** FULL. **Confidence:** HIGH as a false negative.

---

**CASE 17 — ARGENTINA (1998–2001) — FALSE NEGATIVE**
- **Category:** False negative (crisis with economy *also* collapsing — premise violated)
- **P1 trajectory:** Weak political institutions throughout (chronic fiscal indiscipline at provincial level); not a high-base decline.
- **P5 trajectory:** Declining into the December 2001 unrest.
- **Spread:** N/A in the signature's sense.
- **P4 status:** **Collapsing, not masking** — recession from 1998; GDP fell 15.7% from 1998 to the 2001 default (–19.9% cumulatively to 2002); unemployment 21.5%; poverty >45%. The economy was the *driver*, not the mask.
- **Outcome:** December 2001 default, abandonment of convertibility, mass rebellion, five presidents in two weeks.
- **Why this is critical:** A canonical acute crisis that the signature would *not* flag, because P4 was the leading negative indicator — the opposite of the configuration.
- **Data availability:** GOOD (WGI from 1996). **Confidence:** HIGH as a false negative.

---

**CASE 18 — EGYPT (pre-2011) — FALSE NEGATIVE**
- **Category:** False negative (crisis without high-base P1 decline)
- **P1 trajectory:** **Low base, not declining from a high one** — an authoritarian state with entrenched corruption and brutal security services; P1 was already poor, so the "high-base erosion" precondition fails.
- **P5 trajectory:** Repressive stability that masked deep grievances.
- **P4 status:** **Strong** — mid-2000s GDP growth ~7%, 4.1% even in 2008; $35bn reserves in 2010 (8.6 months of imports). The economy genuinely looked healthy — but the Cairo Review called it "a mirage."
- **Outcome:** January 2011 revolution; Mubarak overthrown in 18 days.
- **Why this is critical:** Two of four components were present (strong economy, eroding legitimacy), but **P1 was low to begin with** — so the signature (which requires decline from a HIGH base) would not classify Egypt as a positive, yet an acute crisis occurred. Bounds the signature: it is calibrated for consolidated/high-P1 states, not authoritarian ones.
- **Data availability:** GOOD. **Confidence:** HIGH as a false negative for *this* signature.

---

**CASE 19 — VENEZUELA (1989–1998) — FALSE NEGATIVE**
- **Category:** False negative (institutional decline real, but economy *also* collapsing)
- **P1 trajectory:** Declining institutional quality from a once-high base (Venezuela was Latin America's richest, most stable democracy into the 1970s) — "weakening of democratic governance, poor and declining institutional quality" (Monaldi & Penfold).
- **P5 trajectory:** Declining — the 1989 Caracazo (hundreds killed), two 1992 coup attempts, 1993 presidential impeachment.
- **Spread:** Widening institutionally.
- **P4 status:** **Collapsing, not masking** — a "lost quarter-century"; incomes fell by roughly a third over 25 years; the economy was the leading negative.
- **Outcome:** Caracazo (1989), coup attempts (1992), collapse of the party system, election of Chávez (1998).
- **Why this is critical:** Like Argentina, the institutional erosion was real but ran *alongside* economic collapse — the economy did not mask the rot, so it fails the signature's defining condition.
- **Data availability:** MODERATE (WGI only from 1996; relies on historical/V-Dem/Polity). **Confidence:** MODERATE-HIGH as a false negative.

## A. Category Distribution
- **True positives:** 8 (Turkey, Thailand, Brazil, South Africa, Peru — strong; Tunisia, Sri Lanka, Italy — boundary/partial). Plus the 2 confirmed (Chile, Lebanon) = **10 positives**.
- **True negatives:** 4 (Canada, Germany, Japan, Uruguay).
- **False positives:** 3 (Poland, Hungary, France).
- **False negatives:** 4 (South Korea, Argentina, Egypt, Venezuela).
- **Interpretation:** The roughly 10:3 true-positive-to-false-positive ratio and the clean separation of true negatives suggest a **moderately strong predictor with meaningful false-positive leakage**. It is not an overfitted two-case artifact — the pattern recurs across regions and decades — but it is also not deterministic.

## B. Lead-Time Distribution
- Confirmed: Chile ~5 yrs, Lebanon ~3 yrs.
- New true positives cluster at **~3–6 years** (Turkey ~3 to coup attempt; Peru ~6; Sri Lanka ~3–5; Brazil ~3 to impeachment; South Africa longer at 7–9; Thailand ~5).
- **Conclusion:** Lead times do cluster in the **3–6 year band**, validating the framework's ~3–5 year expectation, with state-capture cases (South Africa, Hungary) running longer or never resolving into a discrete acute event.

## C. Preliminary Sensitivity, Specificity, PPV (qualitative, pending exact scoring)
- **Sensitivity** (of true acute crises in *consolidated/high-P1* states, did the signature fire?): Reasonable but imperfect — it captures Turkey, Thailand, Brazil, Peru, Chile, Lebanon, but **misses South Korea** (acute crisis, no slow signature). Among acute crises in high-P1 states: ~6–7 of 8 ≈ **~75–85% sensitivity**.
- **Specificity** (of stable states, did it correctly *not* fire?): High for clean true negatives (Canada, Japan, Uruguay; Germany mostly) — but **Poland, Hungary and France are false alarms**. Specificity is the signature's **weak point**.
- **Positive predictive value:** Of cases where the full configuration was present (Chile, Lebanon, Turkey, Thailand, Brazil, Peru, Poland, Hungary, France), roughly **6 of 9 produced an acute crisis ≈ ~65–70% PPV** — meaning roughly **one in three positives may be an absorber.**
- **Caveat:** Argentina, Venezuela, Egypt and Tunisia/Italy show the signature correctly should *not* fire (economy failing or P1 low), so excluding them sharpens, rather than weakens, the construct — provided the scoring agent enforces the "P4 strong" and "P1 high-base" conditions strictly.

## D. What This Means for the US/UK Flags
The underlying WGI data **confirm the signature is genuinely present** in both:
- **United States:** Political Stability fell from +1.00 (2000) to -0.19 (2023), recovering only to -0.10 (2024) — at or below the world average (-0.07). Rule of Law fell from +1.69 (2000) to +0.96 (2023–24, an all-time low); Government Effectiveness from +1.93 (2002) to +1.36 (2023–24 low); Control of Corruption from +1.82 (2002) to +1.09 (2024 low). **All four P1/P5 measures are at or near their 1996–2024 lows.** Meanwhile P4 held: real GDP grew ~2.5% (2023) and ~2.8% (2024). This is the configuration, precisely.
- **United Kingdom:** Political Stability fell from +1.10 (2000) to +0.26 (2024, all-time low); Government Effectiveness from +1.84 (2005) to +1.14 (2023 low); Rule of Law from +1.72 (2000) to +1.27 (2023 low); Regulatory Quality from +2.00 (2006) to +1.51 (2024 low). P4 held but was weak (GDP +0.3% in 2023, ~1.0% in 2024) — the masking is *thinner* in the UK because growth is anemic.
- **The decisive question is the absorber/non-absorber distinction.** Poland, Hungary and France prove that the configuration **can** be absorbed. The factors that differentiate absorbers from non-absorbers — none of which the four-pillar signature directly measures — are: **(1) external anchoring** (EU funds/conditionality contributed to Poland's reversal; the US and UK have no such external disciplinarian); **(2) electoral integrity and rotation** (Poland's intact elections enabled reversal; South Korea's Assembly + courts defeated the martial-law gambit in hours); **(3) judicial independence that survives capture attempts; (4) civil-society mobilization capacity; (5) federalism/veto points.**
- **Assessment:** The US flag is the more serious — its Political Stability decline is steeper and it lacks an external anchor like the EU. But the **absorber evidence argues against fatalism**: the US and UK retain strong courts, federalism (US), electoral rotation, and mobilized civil societies — the very assets that let Poland and South Korea absorb shocks. The flag should be read as **materially elevated risk over a 3–6 year horizon, conditional on whether absorber institutions hold.**

## E. Gaps and Limitations
- **No clean true positive with a perfect P4-strong profile besides Peru/Chile/Lebanon:** Several true positives (South Africa stagnant, Brazil weakening, Tunisia/Italy failing) had imperfect P4 — the scoring agent must rule on whether they belong.
- **Reclassification flags:** Tunisia and Italy are arguably false negatives (P4 weak); Egypt fails the high-base P1 precondition; Argentina and Venezuela had collapsing economies — all four should be excluded from sensitivity if the conditions are enforced strictly.
- **Geographic/temporal gaps:** Thin coverage of Sub-Saharan Africa (only South Africa), the Gulf/MENA monarchies, and South/Southeast Asia beyond Thailand/Sri Lanka; few pre-1996 cases survive the WGI-data threshold (Italy, Venezuela, Weimar were not assessable with full WGI). Weimar Germany (1928–33) could not be assessed because its crisis was economy-led (the Depression), likely a false negative.
- **Measurement timing:** WGI is published with a lag and revised backward, so real-time flag-setting (as for the US/UK) carries more uncertainty than the historical cases.
- **The core unmeasured variable:** absorber capacity. Until the MI incorporates a proxy for institutional shock-absorption (courts, federalism, external anchoring, civil-society depth), its positives will continue to include ~1-in-3 absorbers.

## Recommendations
1. **Treat the signature as a screening tool, not a verdict.** Stage 1: confirm all four components with strict thresholds (P1 *high-base* decline, P4 genuinely strong — not merely "not collapsing"). Reclassify Tunisia, Italy, Egypt, Argentina and Venezuela accordingly; this will *raise* PPV.
2. **Add an "absorber sub-index" before relying on the signature for the US/UK.** Score five factors the current pillars miss: judicial-independence trajectory, electoral-integrity/rotation, external anchoring, civil-society mobilization, and veto-point density. Benchmark: if ≥3 absorbers are degrading *concurrently* with the signature, escalate the flag.
3. **Prioritize disconfirming data collection.** The N=21 base still has only 3 clean false positives; aim for 8–10 to pin down specificity. Search specifically for high-P1 democracies that showed the configuration and reversed (candidates: Israel's 2023 judicial-reform crisis, South Korea, Slovakia, Czechia, Romania).
4. **For the US/UK specifically:** monitor the *absorber* variables quarterly, not the pillars (which move slowly and are published with lag). The thresholds that would *change* the assessment: a measurable erosion of electoral-administration integrity, sustained non-compliance by the executive with adverse court rulings, or a collapse in civil-society mobilization capacity. Absent those, the Poland/South Korea precedent supports an "elevated-but-absorbable" rating rather than "pre-collapse."
5. **Have the scoring agent compute exact pillar values and spreads** for all 21 cases and recompute sensitivity/specificity/PPV; treat the qualitative figures here (~75–85% sensitivity, ~65–70% PPV) as provisional.

## Caveats
- All four-component assessments here are **qualitative and preliminary**; exact pillar values await the scoring agent and may reclassify boundary cases (especially Tunisia, Italy, Sri Lanka, South Africa).
- Several "true positives" (Thailand, South Africa) involve states whose "consolidated/stable" precondition is debatable, which weakens the surprise criterion.
- WGI is perception-based and revised backward; real-time application to the US/UK is inherently less certain than retrospective scoring. Note also that different WGI vintages/compilers (TheGlobalEconomy, TradingEconomics, CEIC) show minor year-to-year discrepancies due to the World Bank's annual backward revisions.
- The finding is **neither a vindication nor a debunking of an overfit**: the pattern recurs widely (arguing against overfitting from two cases), but its ~1-in-3 false-positive rate among full-configuration cases means it must be paired with an absorber analysis before it can responsibly drive a US/UK prediction. The goal here was truth about predictiveness, not advocacy for the US forecast — and the honest verdict is *useful signal, not destiny.*
