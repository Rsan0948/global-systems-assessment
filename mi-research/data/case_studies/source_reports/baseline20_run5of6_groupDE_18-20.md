# MODERNIZATION INDEX VALIDATION — RUN 5 OF 6: GROUP D (CASES 18–19) + GROUP E (CASE 20)

## TL;DR
- **Case 18 (Baltics vs Central Asia) CONFIRMS the framework's central claim that institutions (P1) dominate resources (P4):** Latvia (WGI Rule of Law +1.09 in 2024) and Estonia vastly outperform resource-rich Kazakhstan (−0.38) and Uzbekistan (−0.66) on every modernization dimension, despite the Baltics being resource-poor. The four-way P1 ordinal (Estonia > Latvia > Kazakhstan > Uzbekistan) holds with wide, above-margin-of-error gaps.
- **Case 19 (India/Pakistan/Bangladesh) is PARTIALLY CONFIRMED:** India (−0.04) clearly leads, but the Bangladesh−Pakistan gap (−0.96 vs −1.02) sits within the WGI margin of error, so under LIVE (Mod4) the framework correctly DEFERS that sub-ranking to Safeguard B rather than asserting a false ordinal. Bangladesh's August-2024 collapse (Safeguard C) carries forward as confirmed.
- **Case 20 (Myanmar, prospective) is CONSISTENT WITH EVIDENCE THROUGH 2024–25 as a second confirmation of re-suppression-after-porosity (Mod9):** the post-2021 coup produced catastrophic fragmentation (junta controlling ~21% of territory, 75,000+ total conflict deaths, 3.5M+ displaced) far worse than the pre-2011 continuous-military baseline — exactly as the framework's strongest prediction states.

## Key Findings
1. **The P1>P4 showdown is the cleanest result in the run.** Resource endowment did NOT compensate for institutional weakness anywhere. Kazakhstan's oil wealth bought GDP-per-capita (~$13,340 nominal, 2024) but not institutional quality, resilience, or freedom from violence — Kazakhstan's Prosecutor General's office put the January 2022 "Bloody January" toll at 238 dead (including 19 law-enforcement officers), with ~4,500 injured and ~10,000 detained, per RFE/RL.
2. **Safeguard E's 30%-of-GDP threshold (Mod2) is vindicated as a discriminator.** Kazakhstan's total natural-resource rents crossed 30% only in peak-oil years (32.4% in 2005; 33.25% in 2008, per World Bank/CEIC) and sat at 26.84% in 2021 — mostly BELOW threshold. Uzbekistan is similar (peak ~38% in 2006, 20.47% in 2021). Myanmar is far below (~5–11%; 2020 gas rents only 1.8%). None of the three is a mechanically-locked rentier state under the strict LIVE reading — a critical and correct nuance (they resemble Nigeria, not Saudi Arabia).
3. **Mod4 (gap > WGI margin of error) earns its keep in Case 19.** It prevents the framework from over-claiming a Bangladesh>Pakistan ordinal that the data cannot support, deferring instead to Safeguard B. This is the single clearest LIVE-over-STATIC improvement in the run.
4. **Re-suppression-after-porosity (Mod9) now has two confirmations** (Ethiopia/Tigray from Case 11; Myanmar here). Myanmar is the stronger case because the porosity period (2011–2021 democratic opening, two NLD landslides) was longer and the re-suppression more total.
5. **Safeguard I (porosity-with-backstop, Mod10/11) extends cleanly to EU membership** as the ultimate institutional channel-plus-backstop for the Baltics, and plausibly to India's federal system (President's Rule as backstop), though India's case is contested by Modi-era centralization (a possible Mod9 trigger).
