# Long-run golden-age cycles (1850–2012) — multi-dimensional, structurally detected

**Date:** 2026-06-28. Goal: detect golden ages *algorithmically* (no cherry-picking) across every
country, as far back as reliable proxies allow, in **each MI dimension separately** (not collapsed to
one), then pattern-match. **EXPLORATORY** — long-run proxies, not the MI engine; structure, not verdicts.

## Data & proxies (free, long-run; validated where overlap exists)
- **P1 institutions:** V-Dem rule-of-law index (1789–2025). **Validated against WGI P1 in the 1996–2020
  overlap: r=0.87** — a strong proxy.
- **P4 income:** Maddison GDP per capita (1820–2022).
- **P3 human capital:** life expectancy (1543–2023), mean years schooling (1870–2020).
- Detection: a "golden age" = a sustained 10-year climb above a scale-appropriate threshold
  (institutions Δrol>0.15; income Δln(GDP)>0.5 ≈ >5%/yr; life Δ>7yrs), measured uniformly for every
  country-year, **normalized by countries observed** that year (rate, not count — the country count
  changes 10×over the span). 264 economies; robust span ~1900+ (pre-1900 is thin/reconstructed).

## Finding 1 — the dimensions have SEPARATE, non-aligned cycles (collapsing them destroys the signal)
| dimension | golden-age peaks (rate) | driver |
|---|---|---|
| **Institutions** | **1850s** (~15–17%), **1980–85** (20%, 17%) | post-1848 liberal wave; **Huntington's Third Wave** (recovered cleanly, unprompted) |
| **Income** | **1945–70** (12–17%), **1990–2005** (16–29%) | post-war boom; globalization |
| **Life expectancy** | **1940–55** (54%, 37%, 25%) | the mortality revolution (antibiotics/vaccines/public health) |

Contemporaneous correlation of the global rate series: institutions–income **−0.06**, institutions–life
**+0.13**, income–life **+0.21**. **They are different waves at different times**, each driven by its
own force. A single composite MI blends a democratization wave, a growth regime, and an epidemiological
transition into mush — exactly the worry that motivated using all dimensions.

## Finding 2 — NO country-level modernization sequence (an artifact I caught and killed)
The *global* series suggest income leads institutions by ~20–30y (cross-corr +0.60 at lag +20 — post-war
boom → 1980s Third Wave). **But at the country level it vanishes:** a country's institutional golden age
came *after* its income golden age in only **43%** of cases (median −7y). So the global lag is an
**autocorrelation artifact** of two smooth wave-histories, not a within-country law. **No reliable
"material progress then institutions" sequence** — reported as a null, not a finding.

## Finding 3 — the post-war "golden age" was uniquely MULTI-ENGINE
Counting how many engines are "firing" (above each dimension's own median rate) per period: **1940–1970
is the only sustained era where institutions, income, AND life expectancy all fired together.** That is
what made the mid-century singular — not one wave but all three at once. Before and after, engines fire
*alone* or in pairs (institutions alone in the 1980s; income alone ~2000).

## Finding 4 — engines fire together → take turns → go quiet (and the current lull)
The sequence over the modern era: **all-three (1940–70) → single-engine bursts (institutions 1980s,
income 1990s–2000s) → a multi-engine lull (2010s)**. By 2010, income (~3%) and health (~2%) are below
median and institutions only weakly active (~7%). **This lull is real but NOT unprecedented** —
comparable all-quiet stretches occurred ~**1900–1910** and the late 19th century. (Correction: a prior
verbal claim that the current simultaneous drought was *unprecedented* was wrong — 1900–1910 was
quieter.) The distinctive feature now is the *sharp deceleration* from a roaring income engine
(1990–2005) to a broad lull, not an absolute first.

## Honest caveats
- Cross-century comparability is fraught: V-Dem pre-1900 is back-coded with wide uncertainty; income/
  life pre-1900 is sparse; far fewer independent states existed early (the obs≥10 filter mitigates but
  doesn't erase this).
- Thresholds are scale-appropriate but chosen, not derived; the *shapes* of the waves are robust to
  reasonable changes, the exact rates less so.
- The income/life dimensions are partly secular ratchets (everyone rises over 200 years) interrupted by
  wars/depressions — so their "golden ages" are exceptional *accelerations*, not cycles around zero like
  institutions (which genuinely rise and fall).
- One tempting parallel — the 1900–1910 all-quiet preceding WWI — is **n≈1**; noted, not claimed.

## Addendum — the 1900–1910-vs-now parallel, pulled apart (it DISSOLVES)
I flagged "1900–1910 ≈ now" as n≈1 and suggestive. Tested rigorously, it does not hold — and the
record points the other way.

**Opposite textures, not alike.** Decomposing institutional change into simultaneous climb vs decline:
| period | climb | decline | net | texture |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1900–05 | 2–3% | 0–3% | ~0 | **stasis** (frozen at the floor) |
| **now (2010)** | **8%** | **7%** | +1 | **churn** (roiling, nets to flat) |
| 1980s (Third Wave) | 20% | 6% | +14 | surge |
| 1960s | 2–5% | **11–14%** | −12 | **collapse** (worst on record) |
| 1930s | 2% | 8% | −6 | collapse (fascism) |

1900–05 and now share *only* quiet income/health engines; their institutional dynamics are **opposite**
— 1900s frozen, now divergent/polarizing. Same near-zero *net*, 4–5× the *churn*.

**Now is not historically extreme.** Today's institutional-decline rate (7%) merely ties 1910 and is
dwarfed by the **1960s (14% — post-colonial/coup reversal, the worst on record)** and the **1930s (8%)**.
Today most resembles the 1990–2005 post–Cold-War churn, mildly intensifying.

**"Lull precedes rupture" is UNSUPPORTED — it inverts.** The two worst collapses (1960s, 1930s) did NOT
follow lulls; the 1960s reversal followed the *active* post-war churn/decolonization wave. Historically,
ruptures followed **expansion and churn, not calm.** The 1900–1910→WWI thread was a single cherry-picked
case, and the broader record contradicts the "calm before the storm" mechanism it implied. **Retracted.**

**The real signature of the present** is not "pre-rupture lull" but **divergence/churn netting to flat**
— a world where institutional gains and losses run at elevated rates and cancel, distinct from both the
calm 1900s stasis and the 1980s directional surge. (Caveat: V-Dem pre-1900 is smoothed/reconstructed, so
early *churn* may be understated; but the 1960s/1930s collapses register on the same data, and the modern
erosion is corroborated by WGI — so the texture contrasts are real, not pure artifact.)

## What it adds
Confirms golden ages are **real, recurring, and structurally detectable** — but **dimension-specific**:
there is no single "golden age," there are institutional waves, growth booms, and health transitions,
mostly out of phase. The one era they coincided (1945–70) is the historical anomaly we still call *the*
golden age. And the modern multi-engine lull, while not unprecedented, is the sharpest deceleration on
record — which is where the MI's "trust the level, distrust the slope" and the governance-core
stagnation findings reconnect.
