# Gold Standard as External Institutional Substitute — Pre-Registration

**Purpose.** Test whether the ~150-year erosion of the institutional signal
(Findings 2/7; and the dense 19-point curve from Finding 9 Test B1) is explained by
the **international gold standard acting as an external institutional substitute**,
and whether the **post-1971 fiat era** explains the modern re-coupling (Finding 10:
P1↔GDP r 0.770→0.818, 1996→2024).

**Mechanism (distinct from all prior tested hypotheses).** Prior hypotheses proposed
that something changed about *institutions/states/the wealth-governance link* — all
null. This proposes a **system-level monetary regime** that, while in force,
supplied one function institutional quality historically monopolized — **monetary
credibility / international economic trust** — via a commodity anchor, *without
requiring domestic institutional quality*. This partially decoupled institutional
quality from political outcomes (the credibility channel was externally provided).
When the anchor was removed (Nixon, Aug 1971), currency became backed solely by
institutional credibility (central-bank independence, fiscal discipline, legal/
regulatory competence) → the signal **re-couples**. This is neither country-level
(ESI missed it), nor composition (denominator split missed it), nor consequence
change (consequence-elimination missed it): it is a **uniform, bounded,
system-level** regime.

**The falsifiable core.** Unlike the industrialization/financialization story (which
predicts a *smooth monotonic* decline), this hypothesis predicts the erosion curve
has **internal structure that maps onto monetary-regime boundaries**. A smooth,
featureless curve **falsifies** it exactly as it would have falsified
industrialization.

## Test 1 — Does the erosion curve have monetary-regime structure? (THE GATE)

**Predictions (frozen).**
- **P1a** Erosion *slope* steeper (more negative) during **gold** blocks
  (classical gold, Bretton Woods) than during flanking **non-gold** blocks.
- **P1b** **Interwar** block flat or recovering (slope ≥ adjacent gold slopes; not
  steeply eroding) — the anchor was gone, institutions should matter more.
- **P1c** **Post-1971 fiat** block decelerating/flattening/reversing (slope ≥ Bretton
  Woods slope; ideally ≥ 0), consistent with Finding-10 re-coupling.

**Statistical test (frozen).**
1. **Per-regime OLS slopes** of spread~year within each of the 5 blocks (descriptive
   erosion rate).
2. **Segmented (continuous piecewise-linear) regression** with knots at the frozen
   regime boundaries (1870, 1914, 1919, 1944, 1971, 1985) vs a **single linear
   trend** (the Finding-7 model). Compare **AIC and BIC**. (Continuous/segmented, not
   14-parameter discontinuous — N=19 forbids the latter.)
3. **Gold-dummy regression:** spread ~ year + GOLD + year×GOLD. Test the sign of the
   GOLD main effect and the year×GOLD interaction.

**PASS / FAIL (frozen).**
- **PASS (regime structure present):** the segmented model beats single-linear by
  **ΔAIC ≥ 2**, **AND** the directional pattern (P1a) holds (mean gold-block slope
  more negative than mean non-gold-block slope). → proceed to Tests 2–6.
- **PARTIAL:** the directional slope pattern (P1a) holds qualitatively but AIC favors
  linear (structure present but not parsimony-justified), OR the gold-dummy
  interaction is significantly negative while segmented AIC is inconclusive. →
  proceed cautiously, flagged as weak.
- **FAIL:** segmented does not beat linear on AIC **AND** the directional slope
  pattern does not hold (gold blocks not more negative than non-gold; or classical
  gold flat/rising; or post-1971 still eroding as fast). → **report null, STOP.**

Note (disclosed up front): Finding 9-B1 already found a single linear beats every
broken-stick break (1918/1945/1960/1991) on AIC. A 7-regime segmentation adds more
parameters, so the AIC bar is high; the directional pattern is therefore the more
sensitive discriminator and is weighted accordingly in the PARTIAL branch.

## Test 2 — Gold-standard participation as a country-level moderator *(only if gate passes)*

**Participation coding (FROZEN classification, classical era 1870–1914)** — simplified
core/peripheral/non-gold per Officer (EH.Net), Eichengreen (*Globalizing Capital*),
Meissner (2005):
- **Core gold:** GBR, FRA, DEU, USA, NLD, BEL, CHE, SWE, NOR, DNK, AUT (Austria-
  Hungary partial→coded peripheral if ambiguous), CAN, AUS (sterling-linked).
- **Peripheral gold:** ITA, ESP (mostly off), RUS (1897+), JPN (1897+), ARG, and
  other intermittent joiners.
- **Non-gold:** CHN, OToman/TUR, and states never on the classical standard.

**Test.** At the classical-gold anchors (1876–1906, pooled), compute the
structure-vs-wealth spread separately for **core-gold** vs **non-gold** countries
(peripheral reported separately). **Support:** spread erodes more / is lower among
gold members. **Null:** equal. **Contradiction:** stronger erosion among non-gold.
Limitation flagged: per-group onset counts are small.

## Test 3 — P1↔GDP coupling by monetary regime on a constant sample *(only if gate passes)*

Reuse Finding-10's constant historical set (V-Dem rule-of-law vs log Maddison GDP,
same 25-polity balanced set) across anchors 1850/1880/1910/1940/1970 (+ earlier if
covered); overlay regimes. **Support:** coupling weakens during gold eras,
strengthens during non-gold, on the constant sample. **Null:** flat/rising throughout
(replicating Finding 10 — gold does not affect the P1↔GDP relationship; the mechanism,
if any, is P1→stability not P1→wealth). The two pathways are distinct and both
reported.

## Test 4 — Fiat-era recovery (post-1971) *(only if gate passes)*

Post-1971 dense-curve points (1976,1986,1996): decelerating/reversing? Plus
period-pooled structure-vs-wealth spread for conflicts in **1945–1971 / 1972–1995 /
1996–2024** (extending the panel where data allows; capped at 1996 for the committed
curve, with Finding-10's 1996–2024 P1↔GDP as the fiat-era cross-reference).
**Support:** signal weakest in Bretton Woods, strengthening after. Reconcile with
Finding 10.

## Test 5 — Classical gold vs Bretton Woods intensity *(only if gate passes)*

From Test 1's per-regime slopes: **classical gold (1870–1914) erosion steeper than
Bretton Woods (1944–1971)** (classical = fuller substitute; BW = weaker, dollar-only
anchor). **Support:** classical slope more negative. **Null:** equal. **Contradiction:**
BW steeper (→ BW erosion driven by something else, e.g. Cold-War security
architecture).

## Test 6 — Alternative monetary-institution mechanisms *(only if gate passes)*

Discriminate "gold specifically" from "external monetary institutions broadly":
pre-1900 central bank (FROZEN list: GBR 1694, FRA 1800, NLD 1814, ... vs none) and
imperial monetary zone (sterling area, franc zone) erosion patterns. If central
banking / imperial zones explain erosion as well as gold participation, the mechanism
is "external monetary institutions," a variant of the same substitute hypothesis.
