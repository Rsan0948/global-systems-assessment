## RESEARCH BRIEF: 10 randomly-selected PRE-MODERN polity-shock case studies

### What this is
I'm stress-testing a model of how pre-modern states responded to major shocks. I need **10 real
historical cases selected without bias** — randomness and neutrality are the whole job. Do not pick
cases because they're famous, dramatic, well-documented, or "good examples." Do not interpret, rate, or
theorize about *why* things happened. Find real cases by a documented random procedure and report
citable facts. Do **not** search for or read about any "modernization index," "MI," pillar system, or
scoring framework — it would bias you and you don't need it.

### The unit
A **polity-shock episode**: a pre-modern state/empire/kingdom/city-state-system that experienced a
single identifiable major shock, where the aftermath (the following ~1–3 generations) is documented. A
"shock" = imperial overreach/defeat, conquest from outside, succession/dynastic crisis, civil war or
revolt, fiscal/economic collapse, trade-route loss, climate/agricultural/hydraulic failure, or pandemic.

### How to select them (random, documented, auditable)
1. Build a **candidate pool of ≥60 pre-modern polities (c. 3000 BCE – 1800 CE)** from neutral
   general-reference lists of historical states/empires across all world regions — *not* curated for
   outcome or interest.
2. **Number the pool** and use a random-number generator to draw. Document the numbered list and which
   numbers were drawn (I need to see the procedure).
3. Light spread only (re-draw clustered picks): **at most 1–2 per region**, spread across the millennia.
   **Prioritize under-represented regions** (see below) when breaking ties — but draw randomly within
   the open pool, don't hand-pick.
4. Discard a draw only if it (a) hits the exclusion list or (b) has too little evidence to describe its
   pre-shock condition and outcome as facts — then **random-replace**.
5. Deliver **exactly 10**.

### What to report per case (neutral facts only — keep sections separate)
1. **Polity**, **region**, **approximate dates**, and **data quality** (RICH = strong textual record /
   MODERATE / THIN = mainly archaeological).
2. **The shock** and roughly **when** it began.
3. **HEIGHT snapshot** — 3–5 factual, citable sentences on the polity at its peak: how it was
   governed/administered, its economy and trade, its society, its stability. *Describe, do not rate.*
4. **PRE-SHOCK snapshot** — 3–5 factual sentences on its condition *just before* the shock/decline
   (what had changed from the height). *Describe, do not rate.*
5. **OUTCOME (clearly separate block)** — over the following ~1–3 generations, pick the single best-fit
   tag + 1–2 sentences of factual justification + approximate date:
   - **1 — Collapse/fragmentation** (broke apart / lost the core / ceased as a unified state)
   - **2 — Violent rupture** (conquest, civil war, violent dynastic overthrow — survived through violent
     discontinuity)
   - **3 — Discontinuity without collapse** (major non-violent transformation, dynastic replacement, or
     **relocation/contraction of the center** rather than rupture)
   - **4 — Stressed but continuous** (serious crisis/revolt, but the state and order survived and
     recovered)
   - **5 — Absorbed/stable** (weathered with broad continuity)
6. **Sources:** 2–3 citable references (academic histories, peer-reviewed work, authoritative reference).

### Hard constraints
- Pre-modern only; shock onset **c. 3000 BCE – 1800 CE**.
- One clear primary shock per case.
- Enough evidence to state height, pre-shock condition, and outcome as **facts**, not inference (flag
  THIN cases honestly).
- Let the full outcome spectrum appear — do **not** balance the tags; report whatever the draw yields.

### What NOT to do (these void the test)
- Don't cherry-pick famous or clean cases; include obscure and messy ones (flag THIN data, don't drop it
  for being boring).
- Don't balance outcomes or over-engineer the regional mix beyond the light spread rule.
- Don't score, rate, or explain causes — facts, two snapshots, and the one outcome tag only.
- Don't read about any index/scoring framework.
- Keep the OUTCOME block clearly separate from the snapshots (I will read the snapshots first, the
  outcome second).
- Deliver exactly 10, with the documented random-selection list attached.

  framework-aware agent) and the separated OUTCOME block lets us score the pre-shock state **before**

  baseline.** It can corroborate the cross-era *consistency* of the durability gap and container; it
  cannot deliver clean out-of-sample *validation*.
