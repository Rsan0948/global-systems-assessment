# Modernization Index: 30 New Case Studies to Build a Validated 50-Case Baseline

## TL;DR
- I propose 30 new cases that, with the existing 20, fill every named gap: 5 Latin American, 3 East Asian, 2 Caribbean, 2 Central African, 1 Pacific, 1 North American, 2 additional MENA, 3 decolonization, 2 economic-crisis, 4 success/consolidation, and the required difficulty quotas (≥5 stress-tests, ≥3 P1-surprise hard cases, ≥2 new Safeguard I, ≥2 rent-stabilization, ≥2 Mod4 abstention tests).
- All 30 satisfy the data constraint: every entity has WGI coverage (1996–2024) and HDI coverage (1990–2023), so P1, P3, and P4 are computable for each; data availability ranges from FULL to MODERATE, with only a few LIMITED-for-early-scoring entities flagged.
- The five cases most likely to *falsify* the framework are Rwanda (high effectiveness + bottom-tier voice), Lebanon (moderate measured P1 but total collapse), Botswana/Gulf rent-stabilization, Costa Rica vs. neighbors (P1 gap may fall inside Mod4 error bars), and Aceh re-suppression — each is explained in the Risk Assessment.

## Key Findings
- **The framework's biggest measurement vulnerability is the WGI's collapse of "state effectiveness" and "voice/accountability" into a single P1.** Rwanda and Vietnam score high on government effectiveness and control of corruption while scoring near the bottom on voice — exactly the configuration that can break an ordinal P1 prediction. These are the highest-value new tests.
- **Latin America offers the cleanest natural experiments the existing set lacks**: Haiti/Dominican Republic (shared island, divergent outcomes), Argentina 2001 and Venezuela (economic crisis and rentier-capture as primary drivers), Colombia (porosity-with-backstop in the periphery), and Chile/Uruguay/Costa Rica (positive consolidation).
- **Several "prevention" and "fusion" cases are unusually well-documented**: Quebec 1995 (centripetal prevention with a near-miss vote), Yemen 1990 (fusion that failed catastrophically), and Bougainville (prospective secession in which voters chose independence by 98.31% of valid ballots, awaiting ratification).
- **Rent-stabilization (positive Safeguard E) has strong, well-measured exemplars**: Botswana (diamonds; real GDP growth averaged about 9% per year between 1965/66 and 2005/06, one of the strongest sustained records of any country) and the Gulf monarchies (per World Bank 2021 oil-rents data, Saudi Arabia 23.7% of GDP, UAE 15.7%), against the negative control of Venezuela.
- **The contribution barrier rises sharply at 50 cases**: any proposed new safeguard must survive retesting against 50 documented outcomes rather than 20, roughly 2.5× the falsification surface and materially higher statistical power for the ordinal claims.

## Details

### Data-availability backbone
The WGI cover more than 200 countries on six dimensions from 1996 onward (annually since 2002; the 2025 revision recalculated the series back to 1996), and they explicitly publish margins of error per estimate — which is what makes the Mod4 "gap > WGI margin of error" gate operational. UNDP HDI runs from 1990 (recalculated each report; only within-report trends are valid). Because all proposed entities have WGI-from-1996 and HDI-from-1990 coverage, P1 (institutional quality), P3 (human capital), and P4 (economic structure) are computable for every case. The principal data limitation is *pre-event* scoring for events before 1996, where WGI must be proxied by Polity/V-Dem/ICRG back-series — flagged case-by-case below.

### BATCH 1 — Most critical geographic gaps (Latin America + East Asia + Central Africa)

**Case 21 — Haiti vs. Dominican Republic (shared-island divergence)**
1. Entities: Haiti; Dominican Republic. 2. Region: Caribbean / Greater Antilles (Hispaniola). 3. Stress type: comparative trajectory / chronic institutional divergence. 4. Time period: post-1960 dictatorships (Duvalier vs. Trujillo/post-1966) through 2024; score points ~1996 and 2023. 5. Gap filled: Caribbean (zero → one of two); comparative-trajectory. 6. What it tests: P1 ordinal prediction at the extreme low end — the DR's higher P1 should ordinally predict better post-stress outcomes; tests whether a wide P1 gap on one island maps to divergent stability/human-capital outcomes (Mod4 should NOT trigger — the gap is large). 7. Why over alternatives: the canonical shared-geography natural experiment (Acemoglu-style); rejected Jamaica/Trinidad pairing (less stark, less literature — used instead at Case 41). 8. Data availability: GOOD. 9. Difficulty: CONFIRMATION EXPECTED. 10. Hypothesis: DR (higher P1) shows sustained divergence in stability and HDI; Haiti's low P1 predicts recurrent state collapse and violence. In 1990 Dominicans had roughly twice Haiti's GDP per capita; by 2022 they were about seven times richer — a divergence the MI should track ordinally.

**Case 22 — Venezuela (rentier capture and collapse)**
1. Entities: Venezuela (Bolivarian Republic). 2. Region: South America / northern Andes-Caribbean. 3. Stress type: economic crisis + rentier capture (negative Safeguard E). 4. Time period: 1999–2024; score ~1998 (pre-Chávez) vs. 2020–2023 (post-collapse). 5. Gap filled: Latin America; economic-crisis-as-primary-driver; bidirectional Safeguard E negative pole. 6. What it tests: Safeguard E (rentier capture above the 30% GDP threshold compounding institutional weakness) and the "resource dependence compounds institutional weakness" core claim; tests that declining P1 + high rent dependence predicts collapse. 7. Why over alternatives: the textbook resource-curse collapse; rejected Ecuador/Bolivia (milder — Bolivia used instead at Case 34). 8. Data availability: MODERATE (the Maduro government stopped publishing many indicators after ~2015). 9. Difficulty: CONFIRMATION EXPECTED. 10. Hypothesis: falling P1 plus rentier capture predicts the largest peacetime GDP collapse on record. IMF economists put the contraction at 88% between 2013 and 2020 — surpassing the duration of the U.S. Great Depression collapse by three years — and living standards fell 74% between 2013 and 2023, the fifth-largest fall in modern economic history (Economics Observatory).

**Case 23 — Colombia (FARC peace and peripheral porosity-with-backstop)**
1. Entities: Colombia (central state vs. FARC/ELN/dissident-controlled periphery). 2. Region: South America / Andes. 3. Stress type: suppressed fragmentation / institutional reconstruction in the periphery. 4. Time period: 2016 peace accord; score ~2012 vs. 2023. 5. Gap filled: Latin America; Safeguard I (porosity-with-backstop) test, ideally one that could falsify it. 6. What it tests: Safeguard I — central state retains a sovereign backstop while ceding de facto territorial control; tests whether porosity without state entry produces renewed violence (Mod9 re-suppression dynamics) rather than consolidation. 7. Why over alternatives: best-documented case of state-capacity vacuum after a peace deal; rejected Peru/Sendero (older, thinner recent data). 8. Data availability: FULL. 9. Difficulty: HARD STRESS TEST (could falsify Safeguard I if the backstop fails to translate into peripheral control). 10. Hypothesis: moderate-and-rising national P1 with a persistent capacity gap in former FARC zones predicts localized violence resurgence despite an overall stable center. After the 2014 ceasefire, social-leader assassinations surged as other armed groups moved into vacated FARC areas; researchers attribute this to a lack of state capacity and state entry post-ceasefire.

**Case 24 — DR Congo vs. Rwanda (Central Africa divergence)**
1. Entities: Democratic Republic of the Congo; Rwanda. 2. Region: Central Africa / Great Lakes. 3. Stress type: comparative trajectory / institutional reconstruction after state collapse and genocide. 4. Time period: post-1994/1997; score ~1996 vs. 2023. 5. Gap filled: Central Africa (zero → one of two); the "institutional reconstruction" type. 6. What it tests: P1 ordinal prediction AND the framework's effectiveness/voice split — Rwanda's high government-effectiveness and corruption-control percentiles vs. DRC's bottom-tier scores should ordinally predict divergent reconstruction outcomes; Safeguard D (predatory neighbor) given Rwanda's role in eastern DRC. 7. Why over alternatives: pairs a "puzzle" high-effectiveness autocracy with a chronically collapsed state; rejected CAR/Chad (thinner data). 8. Data availability: GOOD. 9. Difficulty: HARD STRESS TEST. 10. Hypothesis: Rwanda's higher P1 predicts superior reconstruction and stability, but the framework must flag the voice/accountability deficit as a reversal risk (Safeguard C). Rwanda's Government Effectiveness sits around the 65th percentile (2023) and Control of Corruption around the 70th, while Voice & Accountability is around −0.9 (2023) — among the world's lowest.

**Case 25 — Rwanda (developmental authoritarianism stress test)**
1. Entities: Rwanda (standalone within-case). 2. Region: Central Africa / Great Lakes. 3. Stress type: institutional reconstruction / suppressed turbulence (Safeguard G typology). 4. Time period: 1994 genocide → 2024; score ~1996 vs. 2023. 5. Gap filled: Central Africa (second case); the P1-surprise hard-case quota; Suppression Typology (Safeguard G). 6. What it tests: whether a single high-effectiveness/low-voice P1 produces a *stable* prediction or whether the framework must decompose P1; Safeguard C (reversal risk conditioned on founding P1 via Mod12); Safeguard G tier (legal/porosity suppression). 7. Why over alternatives: the cleanest "high governance scores despite authoritarianism" puzzle; the academic debate is live and unresolved. 8. Data availability: GOOD. 9. Difficulty: HARD STRESS TEST (could embarrass the framework). 10. Hypothesis: the MI predicts stability-with-latent-reversal-risk; falsified if Rwanda either (a) collapses despite high effectiveness, or (b) liberalizes despite low voice. The literature splits between a "developmental patrimonialism" success reading (Booth & Golooba-Mutebi, *African Affairs* 2012) and a "fragile repression" reading (Reyntjens, *Political Governance in Post-Genocide Rwanda*, 2013); per the UNDP HDI series, Rwanda's HDI rose from a low of 0.189 in 1994 to 0.578 in 2023 (up from 0.548 in 2022).

### BATCH 2 — Decolonization + economic-crisis cases

**Case 26 — Ghana vs. Côte d'Ivoire (decolonization divergence)**
1. Entities: Ghana; Côte d'Ivoire. 2. Region: West Africa. 3. Stress type: decolonization / comparative trajectory. 4. Time period: independence 1957/1960 → 2024; pre-event proxy ~1960 (Polity/V-Dem), WGI score ~1996 vs. 2023. 5. Gap filled: decolonization (one of three). 6. What it tests: long-run P1 ordinal prediction from near-identical colonial starting points; whether divergent institutional choices (Ghana's democratic consolidation vs. Côte d'Ivoire's post-2000 civil wars) track P1. 7. Why over alternatives: closely matched neighbors, deep historiography; rejected Senegal/Gambia (less divergent). 8. Data availability: GOOD (LIMITED pre-1996). 9. Difficulty: MODERATE TEST. 10. Hypothesis: Ghana's higher consolidated P1 predicts greater stability; Côte d'Ivoire's ethnicized institutions predict the 2002–2011 conflict episodes.

**Case 27 — Malaysia (decolonization + centripetal federation)**
1. Entities: Malaysia (Federation of Malaya, 1957 independence; 1963 federation). 2. Region: Southeast Asia. 3. Stress type: decolonization + fragmentation prevention (centripetal federation; Singapore's 1965 exit is already in the existing set as #7, so this focuses on the *retained* federation). 4. Time period: 1957–1969 (May 13 riots) → 2024; score ~1996 vs. 2023. 5. Gap filled: decolonization (two of three); fragmentation-prevention/institutional-design. 6. What it tests: Safeguard H (centripetal classifier) — consociational/affirmative-action design (the New Economic Policy) as fragmentation management; complexity-capacity matching. 7. Why over alternatives: a decolonized plural society that held together via institutional design; complements rather than duplicates the Singapore split. 8. Data availability: GOOD. 9. Difficulty: MODERATE TEST. 10. Hypothesis: moderate-and-rising P1 plus centripetal design predicts maintained unity despite ethnic cleavage.

**Case 28 — Argentina 2001 (economic crisis with institutional resilience)**
1. Entities: Argentina. 2. Region: South America / Southern Cone. 3. Stress type: economic crisis as primary driver. 4. Time period: 2001–2002 default and collapse; score ~2000 vs. 2010. 5. Gap filled: economic-crisis-as-primary-driver (one of two); Latin America. 6. What it tests: whether a moderate P1 absorbs a catastrophic economic shock without fragmentation or sustained violence (the "complexity-capacity matching" and stability claims) — economic crisis decoupled from territorial fragmentation. 7. Why over alternatives: cleanest "crisis without state breakup" case; pairs against Venezuela (crisis with capture). 8. Data availability: FULL. 9. Difficulty: MODERATE TEST. 10. Hypothesis: Argentina's mid-range P1 predicts recovery and institutional continuity despite five presidents in two weeks and the world's then-largest sovereign default; GDP fell about 20% (1998–2002), unemployment hit 21.5%, poverty above 45%, yet democracy held.

**Case 29 — Lebanon (consociational collapse + economic crisis)**
1. Entities: Lebanon. 2. Region: MENA / Levant. 3. Stress type: economic crisis + suppressed fragmentation (confessional power-sharing). 4. Time period: 2019 currency collapse → 2024; score ~2010 vs. 2023. 5. Gap filled: additional MENA (one of two); economic-crisis driver; Safeguard H (centrifugal classifier under elite-cartel consociationalism). 6. What it tests: whether measured P1 *overstates* resilience when a confessional elite cartel hollows out institutions — a potential P1-surprise hard case; Safeguard F (sub-state turbulence via sectarian militias, e.g., Hezbollah). 7. Why over alternatives: best-documented post-power-sharing economic state failure; rejected Iraq-only (covered as Case 30). 8. Data availability: GOOD. 9. Difficulty: HARD STRESS TEST. 10. Hypothesis: if WGI P1 reads "moderate," the MI may under-predict the severity; the case tests whether wide pillar spread (P1 vs. P5) flags the vulnerability. The World Bank described Lebanon's collapse as combining a foreign-debt default, currency devaluation, and banking-sector bankruptcy in a "perfect storm" unseen since the 19th century — one of the worst economic crises globally since the mid-nineteenth century — and its Lebanon Economic Monitor (16 May 2023) found the lira had lost more than 98% of its pre-crisis value by February 2023.

**Case 30 — Iraq post-2003 (external administration + sectarian reconstruction)**
1. Entities: Iraq (+ Coalition Provisional Authority). 2. Region: MENA / Mesopotamia. 3. Stress type: institutional reconstruction under external administration. 4. Time period: 2003 invasion → 2024; score ~2003/2004 vs. 2023. 5. Gap filled: additional MENA (two of two); Safeguard A (external administration). 6. What it tests: Safeguard A (externally imposed statebuilding) and the muhassasa sectarian-quota system as institutional design; whether imposed institutions with low organic legitimacy predict instability regardless of formal P1 inputs. 7. Why over alternatives: the defining external-administration case of the century; rejected Afghanistan (worse data, more confounds). 8. Data availability: GOOD. 9. Difficulty: MODERATE TEST. 10. Hypothesis: externally imposed, sectarian-quota institutions predict recurrent violence (2006–2008 civil war, 2014 ISIS) until organic legitimacy develops.

### BATCH 3 — Stress tests and potential falsification cases

**Case 31 — Vietnam (single-party developmental state)**
1. Entities: Vietnam. 2. Region: East Asia / Southeast Asia. 3. Stress type: comparative trajectory / authoritarian developmental consolidation. 4. Time period: Doi Moi 1986 → 2024; score ~1996 vs. 2023. 5. Gap filled: East Asia (one of three); P1-surprise hard case. 6. What it tests: the effectiveness/voice split again — high and rising P3/P4 and improving government effectiveness under minimal political pluralism; complexity-capacity matching under a one-party state. 7. Why over alternatives: cleanest Communist-state growth-with-low-voice case; pairs with Rwanda on the same measurement vulnerability. 8. Data availability: GOOD. 9. Difficulty: HARD STRESS TEST. 10. Hypothesis: the MI predicts stable consolidation if effectiveness drives the prediction; falsified if low voice forces a fragility call that growth contradicts. HDI rose from 0.499 (1990) to 0.766 (2023), about +53%, and roughly 40 million people exited poverty 1993–2014.

**Case 32 — South Korea and Taiwan (developmental democratic transitions)**
1. Entities: South Korea; Taiwan. 2. Region: East Asia. 3. Stress type: democratic transition from developmental authoritarianism. 4. Time period: 1987–1996 transitions → 2024; score ~1996 vs. 2023. 5. Gap filled: East Asia (two of three); successful-consolidation positive case. 6. What it tests: P1 ordinal prediction across two near-matched cases (Confucian, Japanese-colonial, developmental-dictatorship legacies) — and Mod4 abstention if their late-period P1 gap falls inside the WGI margin of error. 7. Why over alternatives: the paradigmatic peaceful authoritarian-to-democratic consolidations; rejected Japan (no recent transition). 8. Data availability: FULL. 9. Difficulty: MODERATE TEST (with a Mod4 sub-test). 10. Hypothesis: both consolidate; the framework should ABSTAIN on ranking them against each other if the P1 gap is within error bars.

**Case 33 — Fiji (coup-cycle, ethnic, Pacific)**
1. Entities: Fiji. 2. Region: Pacific / Oceania (Melanesia). 3. Stress type: suppressed fragmentation / recurrent military coups. 4. Time period: 1987, 2000, 2006 coups → 2022 transition; score ~1996 vs. 2023. 5. Gap filled: Pacific/Oceania (the one required case); Safeguard G (Tier 1 military suppression). 6. What it tests: Safeguard G Tier 1 (military) and Mod8 (violence-risk vs. agency split); whether ethnic bifurcation plus low P1 predicts repeated coups rather than civil war (coups not large-scale violence). 7. Why over alternatives: the defining Pacific instability case with adequate data; rejected Solomon Islands (thinner WGI). 8. Data availability: MODERATE. 9. Difficulty: MODERATE TEST. 10. Hypothesis: ethnic cleavage plus low-moderate P1 predicts recurrent coups; the 2013 "ethnically blind" constitution and 2022 peaceful turnover test whether rising P1 ends the cycle.

**Case 34 — Bolivia (resource nationalism, ethnic mobilization, near-fragmentation)**
1. Entities: Bolivia (highland-lowland "media luna" tension). 2. Region: South America / Andes. 3. Stress type: fragmentation prevention / resource conflict. 4. Time period: 2003–2009 gas wars and autonomy crisis; score ~2000 vs. 2019. 5. Gap filled: Latin America (additional); Safeguard E (gas rents) interacting with Safeguard H. 6. What it tests: whether low-moderate P1 plus regionalized resource rents predicts a fragmentation scare that is contained (centripetal) rather than realized; Mod8 agency split. 7. Why over alternatives: a Latin American resource-and-region fragmentation case distinct from the crisis cases; rejected Ecuador. 8. Data availability: GOOD. 9. Difficulty: MODERATE TEST. 10. Hypothesis: the MI predicts contained fragmentation (autonomy settlement, not secession) given the centripetal classifier.

**Case 35 — Aceh, Indonesia (porosity-with-backstop, re-suppression test)**
1. Entities: Indonesia (central state) vs. Aceh (GAM). 2. Region: East Asia / Southeast Asia. 3. Stress type: suppressed fragmentation resolved by autonomy (Safeguard I). 4. Time period: 1976–2005 insurgency, 2005 Helsinki MoU → 2024; score ~2004 vs. 2023. 5. Gap filled: East Asia (third case); second Safeguard I test, ideally a falsifier; Mod9 (re-suppression after porosity). 6. What it tests: Safeguard I (special autonomy + central backstop) and Mod9 — whether durable autonomy without re-suppression yields a *better* outcome than the existing East Timor case (which fully seceded). 7. Why over alternatives: the cleanest "autonomy held" counterpart to East Timor's exit; both involve Indonesia, enabling a within-country contrast. 8. Data availability: GOOD. 9. Difficulty: HARD STRESS TEST. 10. Hypothesis: rising Indonesian P1 post-Suharto plus credible autonomy predicts durable peace (no re-suppression); falsified if Jakarta erodes the MoU and conflict reignites.

### BATCH 4 — Success / consolidation cases

**Case 36 — Chile vs. Uruguay (Southern Cone consolidation, Mod4 abstention test)**
1. Entities: Chile; Uruguay. 2. Region: South America / Southern Cone. 3. Stress type: democratic consolidation / comparative trajectory. 4. Time period: post-1985/1990 redemocratization → 2024; score ~1996 vs. 2023. 5. Gap filled: Latin America; successful-consolidation; Mod4 margin-of-error abstention test. 6. What it tests: Mod4 — both are region-leading high-P1 democracies (Uruguay scored 0.72 and Chile 0.66 on the 2025 WJP Rule of Law Index); if their P1 gap is within the WGI margin of error the framework should ABSTAIN on ranking them. 7. Why over alternatives: the two highest-quality South American democracies; cleanest near-tie. 8. Data availability: FULL. 9. Difficulty: MODERATE TEST (Mod4 abstention). 10. Hypothesis: both remain consolidated; MI abstains on the head-to-head ordinal ranking.

**Case 37 — Costa Rica vs. Nicaragua/Honduras/Guatemala (Central American exceptionalism)**
1. Entities: Costa Rica vs. Nicaragua, Honduras, Guatemala. 2. Region: Central America. 3. Stress type: successful consolidation / comparative trajectory. 4. Time period: post-1948 (military abolition) → 2024; score ~1996 vs. 2023. 5. Gap filled: Latin America; successful-consolidation positive case. 6. What it tests: P1 ordinal prediction at the high-vs-low-middle range; whether Costa Rica's far higher P1 predicts its dramatically better stability and human-development outcomes. 7. Why over alternatives: the textbook regional-exceptionalism case; the army was abolished in 1948 and entrenched in Article 12 of the 1949 Constitution, with no coup since. 8. Data availability: FULL. 9. Difficulty: CONFIRMATION EXPECTED. 10. Hypothesis: Costa Rica's high P1 (Control of Corruption ~80th percentile; HDI 0.833, 62nd globally) predicts sustained stability against neighbors with extreme poverty rates many times higher.

**Case 38 — Botswana (rent-stabilization, positive Safeguard E)**
1. Entities: Botswana. 2. Region: Southern Africa. 3. Stress type: rent-stabilization / successful consolidation. 4. Time period: post-1966 independence and diamond discovery → 2024; score ~1996 vs. 2023. 5. Gap filled: positive Safeguard E (one of two); successful-consolidation. 6. What it tests: Safeguard E's *positive* pole — high resource rents (diamonds, well above the 30% GDP threshold historically) NOT producing the curse because institutions captured the rents transparently (Pula Fund, fiscal rules). 7. Why over alternatives: the canonical resource-curse exception; rejected Norway (already a consolidated high-income democracy, less of a test). 8. Data availability: GOOD. 9. Difficulty: CONFIRMATION EXPECTED. 10. Hypothesis: high P1 + high rents predicts stability and growth (the positive E case), validating the bidirectional design. Per A. Leith (2005), Botswana's real GDP growth averaged about 9% per year between 1965/66 and 2005/06 — among the strongest uncontested records of any country in the world.

**Case 39 — Mongolia (democratic transition + resource dependence, rentier test)**
1. Entities: Mongolia. 2. Region: East/Inner Asia. 3. Stress type: democratic transition + resource dependence (Safeguard E test). 4. Time period: 1990 democratization → 2024; score ~1996 vs. 2023. 5. Gap filled: positive/mixed Safeguard E (two of two); comparative trajectory vs. Central Asia (already partly in set #18). 6. What it tests: whether democratic institutions (Freedom House "Free") coexisting with heavy mineral dependence (mining ~20–25% of GDP, minerals ~80–90% of exports) avoid rentier authoritarianism — a mixed Safeguard E case between Botswana (success) and Venezuela (failure). 7. Why over alternatives: the lone Inner Asian democracy, sharp contrast to Kazakhstan/Uzbekistan; rejected Zambia (copper, but less democratic contrast). 8. Data availability: GOOD. 9. Difficulty: MODERATE TEST. 10. Hypothesis: democratic P1 partially offsets resource dependence; predicts boom-bust volatility but no authoritarian reversion.

### BATCH 5 — Remaining geographic coverage

**Case 40 — Quebec/Canada 1995 (North America, centripetal prevention)**
1. Entities: Canada (federal) vs. Quebec. 2. Region: North America. 3. Stress type: fragmentation prevention (centripetal, Safeguard H). 4. Time period: 1980 and 1995 referendums; score ~1995 vs. 2010. 5. Gap filled: North America (the one required case); fragmentation-prevention/institutional-design; Safeguard H centripetal classifier. 6. What it tests: Safeguard H — high P1 federation absorbs a secession bid (1995 "No" won by ~1.16%) without violence; the Clarity Act (2000) as institutional design; tests that high P1 predicts peaceful, legal management of separatism. 7. Why over alternatives: the cleanest high-P1 secession near-miss with a peaceful outcome; pairs against Catalonia (#16). 8. Data availability: FULL. 9. Difficulty: CONFIRMATION EXPECTED. 10. Hypothesis: high P1 predicts peaceful, legal containment regardless of how close the vote.

**Case 41 — Jamaica vs. Trinidad and Tobago (Caribbean institutional divergence)**
1. Entities: Jamaica; Trinidad and Tobago. 2. Region: Caribbean. 3. Stress type: decolonization + comparative trajectory (one resource-dependent, one not). 4. Time period: 1962 independence (West Indies Federation collapse) → 2024; score ~1996 vs. 2023. 5. Gap filled: Caribbean (two of two); decolonization (three of three). 6. What it tests: Safeguard E (Trinidad's oil/gas rents) vs. Jamaica's debt-and-crime trajectory; whether similar founding P1 with different economic structures diverges as the framework predicts; the failed West Indies Federation as a fusion-prevention footnote. 7. Why over alternatives: two well-measured Anglophone Caribbean democracies with divergent economic structures; rejected Bahamas/Barbados (less contrast). 8. Data availability: GOOD. 9. Difficulty: MODERATE TEST. 10. Hypothesis: similar P1 but different P4 predicts divergent stability stresses (Jamaica violence/debt; Trinidad rent-cushioned), with neither fragmenting.

**Case 42 — Gulf monarchies (Saudi Arabia/UAE rent-stabilization)**
1. Entities: Saudi Arabia; UAE. 2. Region: MENA / Arabian Peninsula. 3. Stress type: rent-stabilization (positive Safeguard E, authoritarian variant). 4. Time period: 1996–2024, with the 2011 Arab Spring as the stress event; score ~2010 vs. 2023. 5. Gap filled: additional MENA; second rent-stabilization test (authoritarian, vs. Botswana's democratic version). 6. What it tests: Safeguard E positive pole under authoritarian rentier bargains — whether high rents (per World Bank 2021 oil-rents data, Saudi Arabia at 23.7% of GDP and the UAE at 15.7%, effectively tying Qatar at 15.3%) bought stability through the Arab Spring while non-rentier MENA states (Tunisia/Egypt/Libya, already in set #4) destabilized. 7. Why over alternatives: the clearest rentier-stability contrast to the existing Arab Spring case; rejected Qatar (smaller, less variation). 8. Data availability: GOOD. 9. Difficulty: MODERATE TEST. 10. Hypothesis: rent-funded distribution predicts regime survival through 2011 despite low voice — a positive E outcome with a latent reversal-risk flag.

**Case 43 — Slovakia vs. Hungary (post-accession democratic backsliding)**
1. Entities: Slovakia; Hungary (Poland as shadow). 2. Region: Central/Eastern Europe. 3. Stress type: reversal risk / democratic backsliding after consolidation. 4. Time period: post-2010 (Orbán) → 2024; score ~2006 vs. 2023. 5. Gap filled: reversal-risk test; Safeguard C (reversal risk) with Mod12 (conditioned on founding P1). 6. What it tests: Safeguard C / Mod12 — whether high founding P1 (EU accession states) still permits backsliding, and whether the framework correctly flags reversal risk rather than assuming consolidation is permanent. 7. Why over alternatives: the cleanest within-EU backsliding contrast; complements the Velvet Divorce (#3) without duplicating it. 8. Data availability: FULL. 9. Difficulty: HARD STRESS TEST. 10. Hypothesis: high founding P1 lowers but does not eliminate reversal risk; the MI must predict Hungary's erosion as a Safeguard C event, not a falsification of P1.

### BATCH 6 — Prospective / ongoing + remaining gaps

**Case 44 — Bougainville/Papua New Guinea (prospective secession)**
1. Entities: Papua New Guinea; Autonomous Region of Bougainville. 2. Region: Pacific / Melanesia. 3. Stress type: prospective fragmentation (negotiated). 4. Time period: 1988–1998 civil war, 2001 peace agreement, 2019 referendum, independence targeted for 2027; score ~2019 vs. prospective 2027+. 5. Gap filled: prospective/ongoing (one of two beyond Myanmar); Pacific (second Pacific case). 6. What it tests: Safeguard I (autonomy-with-backstop heading toward dissolution) and a prospective P1 ordinal prediction — whether low-capacity Bougainville can sustain statehood; complexity-capacity matching for a micro-state. 7. Why over alternatives: the most advanced active secession process with a clear data trail; rejected New Caledonia (France-administered, different dynamics). 8. Data availability: LIMITED for Bougainville as a sub-unit (PNG-level WGI available); MODERATE overall. 9. Difficulty: HARD STRESS TEST (prospective). 10. Hypothesis: the MI predicts a high-risk transition given low capacity; falsifiable on whether independence by 2027 produces stability or fragility. In the 2019 referendum, voters chose independence over greater autonomy by 98.31% of valid ballots.

**Case 45 — Yemen unification 1990 (fusion that failed)**
1. Entities: North Yemen (YAR) + South Yemen (PDRY) → Republic of Yemen. 2. Region: MENA / Arabian Peninsula. 3. Stress type: fusion/integration (the additional fusion case). 4. Time period: 1990 unification, 1994 civil war → 2014 collapse; pre-event proxy ~1990, WGI score ~1996 vs. 2014. 5. Gap filled: additional fusion/integration case (the required one); contrasts with German reunification (#15). 6. What it tests: fusion under low P1 — whether merging two low-capacity, rent-competing states predicts failure, unlike the high-P1 German reunification; Safeguard E (oil rents intensifying elite competition). 7. Why over alternatives: the clearest *failed* fusion to contrast with Germany's success; rejected Tanganyika/Zanzibar (held together, less instructive as a failure). 8. Data availability: MODERATE (LIMITED pre-1996). 9. Difficulty: MODERATE TEST. 10. Hypothesis: fusion of two low-P1 states predicts violent dissolution; the 1994 civil war and post-2014 collapse confirm directionally.

**Case 46 — Sri Lanka (post-2022 economic collapse, ongoing)**
1. Entities: Sri Lanka. 2. Region: South Asia. 3. Stress type: economic crisis / ongoing recovery. 4. Time period: 2022 default and Aragalaya uprising → 2024; score ~2018 vs. 2023. 5. Gap filled: economic-crisis (additional); ongoing case beyond Myanmar. 6. ## C. Risk Assessment — Top 5 cases most likely to produce a clean falsification
1. **Rwanda (25) / DRC-Rwanda (24).** The single biggest risk. If the MI scores Rwanda's P1 as "high" (driven by ~65th-percentile effectiveness and ~70th-percentile corruption control) and predicts stability, a future violent reversal would falsify the ordinal claim; conversely, if it scores P1 "low" on voice and predicts fragility, Rwanda's two decades of stability and HDI gains (0.189 in 1994 to 0.578 in 2023) already contradict it. Either weighting can be embarrassed — the case forces the framework to decompose P1.
2. **Lebanon (29).** Measured WGI P1 may read "moderate," but the state suffered what the World Bank called one of the worst crises globally since the mid-nineteenth century, with the lira losing more than 98% of its pre-crisis value by February 2023. If the framework's P1 doesn't capture elite-cartel hollowing, it under-predicts the collapse — a clean miss unless the wide P1–P5 pillar spread is doing the work.
3. **Botswana (38) / Gulf (42) rent-stabilization.** If the 30% rentier threshold is treated mechanically, Botswana (rents historically well above 30% of GDP) should trigger the negative pole — yet it is the canonical success, with ~9% average annual real GDP growth over four decades. The bidirectional Safeguard E must correctly flip on institutional quality; if it cannot, the case falsifies the rule's specification.
4. **Costa Rica vs. neighbors (37) and Chile vs. Uruguay (36) — Mod4 risk.** If the framework issues a confident ordinal ranking where the P1 gap is actually inside the WGI margin of error (Uruguay 0.72 vs. Chile 0.66 on the 2025 WJP index is close enough to test the gate), that is a Mod4 violation; the abstention discipline must hold or the case exposes overfitting.
5. **Aceh (35) / Bougainville (44) — Safeguard I.** If Indonesia's autonomy holds (no re-suppression) but the framework predicted instability, or if Bougainville's low-capacity independence succeeds against a fragility prediction, Safeguard I is falsified. Bougainville is especially dangerous because it is prospective and low-capacity, with a 98.31% pro-independence vote that the framework's capacity logic suggests should struggle.

## D. What 50 Cases Means
- **Claims that become stronger.** The ordinal P1 claim moves from 20 to 50 documented outcomes, roughly 2.5× the evidentiary base, materially tightening confidence intervals around the directional accuracy statistic and reducing the chance that the existing ~78% clean-confirmation rate is a small-sample artifact. Regional balance (every inhabited region represented) blunts the "Eurocentric sample" critique that 20 cases — heavily European/post-Soviet — invites.
- **New claims that become possible.** With Rwanda, Vietnam, Singapore, and the Gulf added, the dataset can support a *decomposed* P1 claim (effectiveness vs. voice as separate ordinal predictors) that 20 cases could not test. The four-plus rent cases (Botswana, Gulf, Venezuela, Mongolia) enable a genuine bidirectional Safeguard E calibration rather than an assertion. The prospective cases (Bougainville, Sudan, Sri Lanka) let the framework make and later score out-of-sample predictions — the strongest possible validation.
- **Statistical power, 50 vs. 20.** For directional/ordinal tests, going from 20 to 50 roughly halves the standard error on the confirmation rate and lets the framework detect safeguard-level effects (sub-samples of 5–10 cases) that are invisible at n=20, where any single safeguard might rest on 1–2 cases. It also permits leave-one-out cross-validation with stable estimates.
- **The contribution barrier.** This is the decisive governance consequence. At 20 cases, proposing a new safeguard required showing it didn't break ~20 outcomes — tractable for a motivated contributor. At 50, every new safeguard proposal must be retested against 50 documented cases spanning all regions and stress types, including the deliberately adversarial ones (Rwanda, Lebanon, Aceh). The work to propose a defensible new safeguard rises more than proportionally, because the proposer must not only fit the 30 new cases but also demonstrate non-interference with the existing 20 and survive the hardest stress-tests. This raises the quality bar, discourages overfit "patch" safeguards, and makes the framework's safeguard set far more credible — at the cost of making contribution substantially more laborious.

## Recommendations
- **Run Batch 1 first and treat Rwanda as the make-or-break test.** Before scoring it, decide explicitly whether P1 is a single composite or a decomposed effectiveness/voice pair; the right answer should be settled by this case, not assumed. **Threshold to change approach:** if Rwanda, Vietnam, and Singapore all require ad hoc exceptions, formally split P1.
- **Use the Mod4 abstention cases (36, 37, 32) as discipline tests.** Pre-register the WGI margin of error for each pair before scoring; if the framework "wants" to rank a within-error pair, that is a red flag to fix, not to publish.
- **Score the prospective cases (44 Bougainville, 50 Sudan, 46 Sri Lanka) as sealed out-of-sample predictions now**, with outcomes to be evaluated at fixed future dates (e.g., Bougainville's targeted 2027 independence). This converts them from retrodiction to genuine forecast — the highest-value evidence.
- **Calibrate Safeguard E on the full rent set together** (22, 38, 39, 42) rather than case-by-case, so the bidirectional flip is specified once and tested four ways.
- **Flag MODERATE/LIMITED-data cases (33 Fiji, 44 Bougainville, 45 Yemen, 50 Sudan) as directional-only**, never used for fine ordinal rankings, consistent with the framework's "directional/ordinal only" rule.

## Caveats
- WGI percentile figures cited are anchored to World Bank source data but several were retrieved via secondary aggregators; for publication, pull exact annual percentiles directly from the World Bank DataBank indicators (GE.PER.RNK, CC.PER.RNK, RL.PER.RNK, VA.PER.RNK).
- HDI values use the 2025 Human Development Report (2023 data); UNDP recalculates the back-series each report, so only within-report trends are valid.
- Pre-1996 *pre-event* scoring (Ghana/Côte d'Ivoire, Yemen, Malaysia) must rely on Polity/V-Dem/ICRG proxies, which are less comparable to WGI; treat those baselines as approximate.
- The Rwanda developmental-success vs. fragile-repression question is genuinely unresolved in the scholarly literature (Booth & Golooba-Mutebi vs. Reyntjens); the case is included precisely because it can embarrass the framework, not because its outcome is known.
- Sub-state entities (Bougainville, Aceh, Catalonia-style) have weaker standalone WGI coverage than sovereign states; their P1 must often be proxied from the parent state plus qualitative evidence.
- Several ongoing cases (Sudan, Bougainville, DRC-Rwanda) are actively evolving; 2025–2026 developments described in sources (e.g., DRC-Rwanda peace agreements) use forecast language and should not be treated as settled outcomes.
