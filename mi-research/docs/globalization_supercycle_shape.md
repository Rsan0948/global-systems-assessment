# The globalization-supercycle SHAPE — boom→rapid-fall, and what actually decides the sequel

**Date:** 2026-06-28. Correcting a framing error: I had tested whether *now* matches the *static
texture* of 1900–1910 (it doesn't — that was "dissolved" in `longrun_golden_age_cycles.md`). But the
real claim was always about the **dynamic shape** — a large sustained global golden age followed by a
relatively rapid fall to low growth — and "now" maps not to a quiet 1900–1910 but to the **end of a
globalization supercycle**. Tested on that frame, the pattern is real. EXPLORATORY (Maddison + V-Dem
proxies); MI engine untouched.

## The boom→rapid-fall shape is REAL and RECURS (~1.5pp/decade, three times)
Global median 10-yr GDP-pc growth, peak → 10 years later:
| episode | peak growth | +10y | deceleration |
|---|---|---|---|
| First globalization / Belle Époque | 1913: +1.8%/yr | 1923: +0.5% | **−1.3pp** |
| Post-war golden age (trente glorieuses) | 1973: +3.3%/yr | 1983: +1.4% | **−1.9pp** |
| Second globalization | 2007: +3.4%/yr | 2017: +2.0% (→ 1.2% by 2020) | **−1.4pp** |

Three global growth supercycles, each ending in a deceleration of nearly the same magnitude. The shape
is not imagined — it recurs. (This is the right comparator for "now": the *end of the second
globalization*, ~mapping to ~1913–1920 of the first.)

## But the shape does NOT determine the sequel — the sequels diverged wildly
- 1913 → **WWI, interwar collapse, WWII** (catastrophe).
- 1973 → stagflation, then a **survivable regime-shift** (no great-power war; growth recovered).
- 2007 → GFC, secular stagnation, populism — **ongoing, unknown.**

Same shape, one catastrophe and one manageable transition. So you **cannot** read the outcome off the
deceleration. Something modulates it.

## The discriminator: the institutional CONTAINER's trajectory *during* the fall
Net institutional change (climb-minus-decline rate) across each growth-deceleration window:
| episode | institutions during the fall | container | outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1910–1925 (Belle Époque fall) | **net −1pp, trending to −6pp by the 1930s** | **collapsing → brittle** | rupture (WWI/interwar) |
| 1973–1985 (post-war fall) | **net +8pp (the Third Wave SURGING)** | **strengthening → robust** | absorbed |
| 2005–2018 (now) | **net +2pp on V-Dem, but eroding underneath** (WGI: 50% of cores declining, hollow stability) | **flat-to-eroding → ambiguous, leaning brittle** | ? |

**This is the project's core thesis, at world-system scale:** it is not the growth *slope* that decides
whether a deceleration ruptures — it's the institutional *level/structure* (here, its trajectory). The
1970s deceleration was absorbed because institutions were *strengthening* through it (democratization);
the 1913 deceleration ruptured because institutions *collapsed* alongside it. The deceleration was the
same both times.

## Where that puts "now" — honestly
Now is a **normal, recurring globalization deceleration** (nothing unusual about the fall itself) — but
it is landing on a container that, unlike the 1970s, is **not strengthening**: V-Dem net is barely
positive, and our finer modern data (WGI governance core flat-to-eroding in half of countries, hollow
stability masked by the human-capital ratchet) shows active erosion the coarse proxy misses. So the
defensible concern is **not** "we're decelerating like before WWI" — decelerations are normal and the
1970s one was fine. It is: **we are decelerating without the institutional strengthening that absorbed
the last comparable deceleration, and with erosion underneath.** That is the brittle-container
configuration, not the robust one.

This also reconnects to the fragmentation program's mechanism: the container is the system's capacity to
*vent* pressure. A strengthening container (1970s) vents the deceleration's stress (new institutions,
peaceful transitions); a brittle/eroding one (1910s; now) has nowhere to put it.

## Micro-scale: can country-level boom-busts expand n=3? (directional, weak)
Each national boom→bust is a micro-realization of the supercycle shape. Detecting them systematically
(255 episodes, 1910–2010: a GDP-growth peak followed by a sharp deceleration), with container = rule-of-
law level at the peak and **rupture = a large regime swing in EITHER direction** (an order breaking
toward democracy *or* chaos — a direction-agnostic upheaval; a "decline-only" proxy is blind to
ruptures that democratize, e.g. Indonesia/Korea 1997):
- LOW container: **17%** upheaval (n=127) vs HIGH container: **12%** (n=128) — right direction
  (low institutions rupture more) but **z=+1.1, weak, not significant**, and partly a floor effect.
- So country-level episodes give **directional corroboration, not powered confirmation** — n=3 does not
  cleanly multiply into a strong law.

**Where it IS clean: within-shock natural experiments** (one shock, several countries — controls for the
shock, varies the container). 1997 Asian crisis: same capital-flight shock; **Indonesia (rol 0.05)
ruptured (Suharto fell, swing 0.49); Malaysia & Thailand (rol ~0.42) absorbed.** The discriminator
holds with the shock held fixed. (Conceptual note: "rupture" = discontinuous regime change either way;
ruptures that democratize make institutional indices *rise*, so direction-agnostic upheaval is required.)

## Honest limits
- **n=3** supercycles, **n=2** clean outcomes (one catastrophe, one benign) — the discriminator is a
  *mechanism-consistent reading*, not a statistically powered law. It cannot be.
- The growth-shape recurrence is solid (Maddison); the "container" contrast rests on V-Dem net + modern
  WGI, with V-Dem pre-1940 smoothed (the 1910s collapse is understated in the 1910–25 window, sharpening
  only into the 1930s).
- "Ambiguous, leaning brittle" for now is a judgment that the modern erosion (well-evidenced) outweighs
  the mildly-positive coarse V-Dem net — reasonable, not certain.
- This is not a prediction of catastrophe. It is the precise statement of *why* the present is concerning
  and *what would change the verdict*: a genuine institutional strengthening through the deceleration —
  a new Third-Wave-scale container repair — would move it from the 1913 column toward the 1973 column.
