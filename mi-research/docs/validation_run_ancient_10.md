# Out-of-sample run — 10 blind-selected ancient cases (RESULT)

**Date:** 2026-06-28. Blind-SELECTED by a framework-naive agent via documented RNG
(`ancient_validation_brief.md`); I scored pre-shock institutions (P1) from the HEIGHT + PRE-SHOCK
snapshots **before revealing the OUTCOME block** (the most blindness this tier allows). Lowest-confidence,
interpreter-scored, hindsight; firewalled from the 213C/0F baseline. Cases: Mitanni, Neo-Assyria,
Xiongnu, Athens, Teotihuacan, Uyghur Khaganate, Chola, Pagan, Champa, Poland-Lithuania.

## The fatal limitation (flagged by the agent, confirmed here): near-zero outcome variance
9 of 10 ended in collapse/violent rupture (Tag 1/2); only Poland-Lithuania survived (Tag 4); Tags 3 & 5
= 0. Random "pre-modern polity-shock" draws are dominated by episodes remembered *because* they ended
states (survivorship/memorability bias). And **6 of 10 are conquest-from-outside** — the same exogenous
blind spot the modern run hit. With no outcome variation and the wrong shock-mix, this **cannot validate**
the framework; it can only offer a consistency anecdote.

## The one signal it can carry (weak, anecdotal)
**Survivors' mean pre-shock institutions ≈ 4.5 vs ≈ 2.4 for those that ceased to exist.** The two
clearly highest-institution polities (Poland 5, Athens 4) survived their shocks; the lower-institution
ones ceased. Poland — the most institutionally developed of the ten — survived the *worst* shock in the
set (the Deluge, ~⅓ of the population). Directionally consistent with the container/P1-ordinality thesis.

But: **n=2–3 survivors; "survival" is generous** (Athens lost its empire; Xiongnu merely dispersed); the
scoring is my interpretation; the outcome barely varies. This is consistency, not a test.

## Cross-temporal mechanism notes (qualitative, from the snapshots)
- **Container erosion is nearly universal pre-shock** — succession war (Mitanni, Neo-Assyria, Xiongnu),
  factional assassination (Uyghur), feudatory fraying (Chola), fiscal hollowing by tax-free temple land
  (Pagan), civil wars + isolation (Champa). Present in *all*, including the survivor (Poland: leaderless
  at the king's death, oppressed Cossacks) — so it is **necessary-but-non-discriminating** here; the
  discriminator for survival was the institutional *level* (Poland highest), not the presence of erosion.
- **Confederacy fragility** recurs (Xiongnu, Mitanni, Champa — loose hierarchies that fragmented fast),
  consistent with the ancient-25 finding.

## Verdict — and the parallel with the modern run
Like the 30-case modern run, this draw is **directionally consistent but cannot validate**: both
independent blind draws (a) point the right way (institutions help) and (b) land in the same blind spot
(exogenous shocks, here plus near-zero outcome variance). The consistent *direction* across two
independent blind draws is modestly encouraging; the consistent *blind-spot/variance* problem is the
design lesson, sharper than for the moderns:
- **A testable historical sample must be selected on the SHOCK, not the OUTCOME** — include polities that
  faced a comparable shock and *survived*, not only the ones remembered for dying. Memorability bias
  otherwise guarantees a near-all-collapse sample with nothing to discriminate.
- Stratify by endogenous vs exogenous shock (same lesson as the moderns).

## Caveats
- All scores are mine, interpretive; Teotihuacan is THIN (political form and even the shock are inferred).
- Three cases flip tags on the unit-of-analysis choice (Xiongnu, Athens, Champa — empire vs polity).
- Hindsight unavoidable; this is a post-hoc consistency check, firewalled, lowest-confidence.
