# V2: Honest restatement of the P1-centrality claim

**Status:** ADOPTED in V2. Supersedes the V1 weighting rationale (not the ordinality finding).

## What V1 claimed
> "The data independently pushed P1 from 25% to 34%, confirming institutional quality as the most
> central variable."

## What V2 states (honest)
At **85 countries in 2018**, P1 appeared most central — which is what produced the V1 0.34 weight.
At **143 countries across three time points (2012/2018/2024)**, the most-central pillar **rotates**,
and all five pillars correlate in a tight **0.71–0.80 band**. **The V1 centrality finding was an
artifact of the specific sample and time point**, not a stable structural fact. Accordingly, V2
drops correlation-derived weights for **equal weights** (see `v2_weighting_decision.md`).

## What is PRESERVED (and why it's independent of the weighting)
The retrodiction finding — **pre-event P1 ordinally predicts post-stress outcomes** — stands, and is
restated as an empirical result that does **not** depend on the weighting rationale:

> **Regardless of how the pillars are weighted, the entity with higher institutional quality fared
> better in every tested case where the gap exceeded the WGI margin of error.**

This is directly supported by the V2 A-vs-B weighting retrodiction: equal and time-varying weighting
produced **identical** mechanical verdicts (zero per-case differences). The ordinal signal lives in
**P1 itself**, not in its weight — so re-weighting cannot manufacture or destroy it. (P1 is a pillar
input; the a-trajectory verdict compares pre-event P1 rank to post-event outcome rank.)

## The rotation as an open question (flagged for V3)
Why does the most-central pillar rotate across time points (P2-leaning ~2012, P1 ~2018, P3 ~2024)?
**Open research question for V3:** does the *binding constraint* on modernization shift over time in
response to global macro conditions (e.g. post-GFC innovation premium → institutional premium →
human-capital premium)? V2 does not assume it does (equal weights, time-varying rejected on the
tie-breaker); it records the rotation as a phenomenon to investigate, not a feature to exploit yet.

## Net
- Weighting rationale: **corrected** (rotation/artifact acknowledged; equal weights adopted).
- Ordinality finding: **preserved and strengthened** (shown weighting-independent).
- Rotation: **flagged for V3**.
