## Test 1 (THE GATE) — does the erosion curve map onto monetary regimes? — NO

Primary dataset: the 19-point dense curve from Finding 9 B1, regenerated by the
**committed, unmodified** `erosion_component_B.dense_curve()` (structure = V-Dem
rule-of-law, wealth = log Maddison GDP, outcome = conflict onset in 25y, spread =
structure_AUC − wealth_AUC).

**Per-regime erosion slopes (spread per year):**

| block | span | gold? | slope | mean level | n | p |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| pre-gold | 1816–1866 | no | −0.00146 | +0.174 | 6 | 0.47 |
| **classical gold** | 1876–1906 | **GOLD** | **+0.00076** | +0.132 | 4 | 0.58 |
| interwar disruption | 1916–1936 | no | −0.00082 | +0.104 | 3 | 0.61 |
| **Bretton Woods** | 1946–1966 | **GOLD** | −0.00202 | +0.055 | 3 | 0.23 |
| **post-1971 fiat** | 1976–1996 | no | **−0.00382** | +0.019 | 3 | **0.025** |

**Every prediction fails, most reverse:**
- **P1a — gold steeper than non-gold: REVERSED.** Mean gold slope **−0.00063** vs mean
  non-gold slope **−0.00203**. Non-gold periods erode **~3× faster** than gold periods
  — the opposite of the hypothesis.
- **Classical gold (the flagship predicted erosion zone) does not erode at all** —
  slope **+0.0008** (flat/slightly rising). The signal was *stable* precisely when the
  fullest external anchor was in force.
- **P1b — interwar recovery: REVERSED.** Interwar slope −0.00082 is *more negative*
  than classical gold's +0.00076; no recovery when the anchor collapsed.
- **P1c — post-1971 fiat recovery: REVERSED.** Post-1971 has the **steepest** erosion
  of any block (−0.00382) and is the **only statistically significant** slope
  (p=0.025). The signal erodes *fastest* after the anchor was removed — the exact
  opposite of the predicted re-coupling.

**Model comparison (the pre-registered statistical gate):**

| model | params | RSS | AIC | BIC |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| single linear (Finding-7 model) | 2 | 0.03020 | **−64.53** | **−62.64** |
| segmented, knots at regime boundaries | 8 | 0.02625 | −55.19 | −47.63 |

The regime-segmented model fits marginally better in raw RSS but is **crushed on
AIC (Δ = −9.34) and BIC (Δ = −15.01)** — the modest RSS gain does not remotely
justify six extra parameters. The curve is a **smooth continuous decline** (Finding
9-B1 already found single-linear beats every broken-stick break; this extends it to
the full regime segmentation). Per the pre-registration: *"Smooth continuous decline
with no regime structure: fail."*

**Gold-dummy regression** (`spread ~ year + GOLD + year×GOLD`): coef_GOLD = +0.0028
(**p = 0.90**), coef_year×GOLD = −0.00002 (**p = 0.97**). Gold-era membership has no
detectable effect on either the level or the slope of the spread.

**Verdict: FAIL.** Gate not passed. Stop.

## Reconciliation with Finding 10 (mandatory)

Finding 10 established modern **P1↔GDP re-coupling** (r 0.770→0.818, 1996→2024). Does
this gate contradict it? **No — the two measure different pathways, and the
distinction sinks the hypothesis rather than saving it.**

- Finding 10's re-coupling is on the **wealth pathway** (P1↔GDP correlation).
- This gate is the **political-outcome pathway** (structure-vs-wealth spread for
  predicting *conflict rupture*).

The hypothesis needed the **fiat era to re-couple the political-outcome signal** (the
erosion curve is the political-outcome pathway). Instead, the political-outcome spread
**keeps eroding through 1996, fastest of all in the fiat block**, while only the
separate P1↔GDP correlation re-couples. So the fiat era does *not* reverse the erosion
this hypothesis is about; the re-coupling Finding 10 saw is confined to the wealth
pathway and, crucially, its **timing does not map to 1971** — the political-outcome
erosion accelerates after 1971 rather than reversing. The two findings are
consistent; together they show the monetary-regime story does not hold.

## Tests 2–6 — NOT RUN (pre-registered stop rule)

The pre-registration (rule 2) and Test-1 interpretation guide are explicit: *"Test 1
is the gate. If it fails, report the null and STOP. Do not proceed to Tests 2–6
searching for a positive."* The gate failed with reversed directional predictions and
a decisive AIC/BIC preference for the featureless linear trend. Running gold-
participation moderation (T2), constant-sample P1↔GDP-by-regime (T3), fiat recovery
(T4), intensity gradient (T5), or alternative-monetary-institution discrimination
(T6) would be fishing for a positive against a failed gate. They are not run.

## Bottom line

The erosion curve is smooth with respect to monetary regimes; where it has any
regime-linked feature at all, that feature is **backwards** relative to the
hypothesis (classical gold flat, fiat era steepest-and-significant). The gold
standard did not function as a detectable external institutional substitute in the
erosion curve, and the fiat era did not re-couple the political-outcome signal.

This is the **eleventh** hypothesis tested against the 150-year erosion and the
eleventh to fail on the longitudinal curve: not composition (9-A), not
consequence-elimination (9-B), not perception-lag (9-C), not external support
(Finding 8), not wealth–institution decoupling (Finding 10, 8 tests), and not
monetary-regime substitution (Finding 11). The erosion is **real, smooth, and its
cause remains genuinely open.** The smoothness itself is now the most informative
constraint: whatever drives the erosion operates **continuously**, not in the
discrete regime-linked steps that a monetary-substitute (or any punctuated-cause)
mechanism would produce — which points away from the entire family of
regime/event-based explanations and back toward a slow, monotone process
(measurement-regime maturation / variance compression / the changing character of
the conflict outcome — Finding 9's candidate residuals, still awaiting their own
pre-registration).
