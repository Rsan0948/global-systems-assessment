## ★ Headline: the framework's FIRST falsification (Case 36, Chile vs. Uruguay)
Chile had the higher **pre-event P1 (0.759 vs 0.652, gap 0.107)** — *just* outside the Mod4 margin
(0.10), so the framework makes an ordinal call: Chile > Uruguay. **It was wrong.** Chile's MI
**declined** (0.747→0.691) while Uruguay's held (0.726→0.716) — Uruguay ended higher. Predicted-top
≠ outcome-top → **a_trajectory FALSIFIED**.

This is coded as a clean falsification (not softened to PARTIAL) on purpose — it is the single most
important signal in the 51-case run and the prime input to V3:
- It sits **only 0.007 outside the Mod4 margin** → either the margin is slightly too tight (0.11–0.12
  would convert this to an honest abstention), **or** Uruguay is a genuine exception (lower measured
  P1, better realized trajectory — its stability/cohesion may compensate). V3 should adjudicate.
- It does **not** overturn the bounded claim ("higher P1 fares better *when the gap exceeds the
  margin*") so much as probe where that boundary sits. Honest directional accuracy is now **99.7%
  (1 falsification / 290 predictions)**, no longer a perfect record — which is more credible, not less.
