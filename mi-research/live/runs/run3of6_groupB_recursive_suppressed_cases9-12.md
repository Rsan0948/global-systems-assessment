# Modernization Index Validation — Run 3 of 6: Group B Cases 9–12 (Recursive and Suppressed Fragmentation)

## TL;DR
- Across all four cases, the **STATIC** framework's core P1-ordinality claim survives the recursive/suppressed cases, but only because the **LIVE** modifications (especially Mod 4's WGI-margin-of-error gate, Mod 5's capacity-gate promotion, and Mod 6's centrifugal/centripetal classifier) repair the two places STATIC fails outright — the directional-violence problem (Case 10) and the floor-P1 false-fragmentation problem (Case 12). LIVE outperformed STATIC on Cases 10, 11, and 12 and tied on Case 9.
- The single biggest empirical finding is that **Safeguard E (Mod 2) does NOT apply to Nigeria**: Nigeria's oil rents were ~17.8% of GDP in 1996 and fell to ~8.8% by 2018 (total resource rents ~22% → ~11%), never crossing the 30% inversion threshold in any benchmark year. Nigeria's 55-year unity is therefore **not** an oil-rent-stabilization story in the Timor-Leste sense; it is better explained by Safeguard B + federal porosity (Tier 3) than by Safeguard E. This is the most important correction this run produces.
- The **"re-suppression after porosity"** prediction (Case 11) is **CONFIRMED**: Abiy's 2018–2023 dismantling of ethnic federalism (a Tier 3 porosity system operating since 1995) preceded both the Tigray War (300,000–600,000 dead) and the Amhara Fano insurgency (2023–), exactly the "worse than continuous suppression" pattern the framework predicts. This is the strongest single confirmation in the run.

## Key Findings
1. **LIVE beats STATIC on 3 of 4 cases; ties on 1.** Modifications 4, 5, and 6 earned their keep. Modification 2 (Safeguard E inversion) was correctly *triggered* for Timor-Leste and correctly *withheld* for Nigeria — a "negative confirmation" that validates the 30% rule's discriminating power. Modification 7 (Bangladesh/Safeguard C analogy) did NOT trigger for Nigeria because Nigeria lacks the high-growth half of the pattern.
2. **The directional-violence tag (Case 10) is the clearest STATIC failure.** In East Timor, lethal violence came from the *departing higher-P1 power* (Indonesia), not from the low-P1 fragment. STATIC's Prediction 2 ("low P1 at stress predicts violent outcomes") points at the wrong actor. LIVE's Safeguard D (predatory neighbor / exogenous shock) plus the explicit directional tag correctly reassign agency.
3. **Floor-P1 ordinality is unreliable and must defer to the capacity gate.** Cases 11 and 12 both sit near or below the bottom-third WGI Rule of Law floor. Mod 4 (margin-of-error gate) and Mod 5 (capacity gate first) correctly predict catastrophic outcomes regardless of fragment count, which is what materialized.
4. **Recursive fragmentation (Cases 9, 11) is handled adequately by the directional framework** — Kosovo (2nd-order from Yugoslavia) and Tigray (Ethiopia having already lost Eritrea in 1993) both behaved as the spread/instability signals indicated, though the framework cannot and does not claim to have predicted *timing or trigger*.
5. **Safeguard A (external administration) is essential for both Kosovo and Timor-Leste.** Measured WGI for both reflected UNMIK/EULEX and UNTAET administration, not indigenous capacity. STATIC flags this; LIVE (Mod 1) operationalizes the discount, which is more defensible.

## Details

### Methodological note on scoring
The MI is explicitly **directional/ordinal** (Core Prediction 5; reinforced by Mod 3). I therefore score predictions as CONFIRMED / PARTIALLY CONFIRMED / FALSIFIED on ordinal grounds and do **not** report spurious precise MI point scores for countries not in the longitudinal dataset, because the exact normalization constants are not recoverable from public data without the proprietary pipeline. Where I give pillar directions I mark them as estimates. Missing indicator values are reported as missing, never interpolated. All WGI figures are World Bank 2025-revision "Estimate" values (−2.5 to +2.5 scale) unless noted; vintage discrepancies are flagged.

- **G:** Contested. Kosovo's independence was a unilateral declaration; the ICJ's 22 July 2010 advisory opinion held (10–4) that "the declaration of independence... did not violate international law." Best classified as a *shift out of suppression toward recognized (if partial) statehood* rather than a Tier 1/2/3 suppression event.

**4. Predictions (both versions)**

- (b) Violence: Low-intensity, episodic, not large-scale war (both). 
- (c) Convergence/divergence: Divergence — Serbia pulls ahead economically; Kosovo stays aid/remittance-dependent (both). 
- (d) Primary dimension: Kosovo's weakness = P4 (economic independence) + P1; Serbia's risk = P1/P5 reversal. 
- (e) Recursive fragment count: manageable; no further fragmentation predicted directionally. 
- (f) Reversal risk: Serbia (democratic backsliding) — both flag; LIVE flags more strongly. 
- (g) Suppression tier: not a clean tier; LIVE explicitly reclassifies as exit-from-suppression.

**5. Post-event data (2012/2018/2024)**
Kosovo: recognized by at least 110 of 193 UN member states (57%) as of 19 December 2025 (per Wikipedia, "International recognition of Kosovo"; Kosovo's foreign ministry claims 118–120, while Serbia claims the figure has fallen to 84 after alleged withdrawals). Steady GDP growth (~50% per-capita income rise since independence per World Bank), reduced aid dependence, but still highest-unemployment in region. Serbia: GDP per capita PPP rose to ~$26,884 by 2024; sustained backsliding; Banjska 2023 incident; EU accession stalled.

**6. Scoring**
- (a) CONFIRMED (both). (b) CONFIRMED (both) — no return to war. (c) CONFIRMED (both). (f) CONFIRMED, LIVE stronger. (g) STATIC ambiguous / LIVE CONFIRMED.

**7. Case findings**
Recursive (2nd-order) fragmentation did not break the framework. Both versions got the ordinal call right. LIVE's Mod 1 discount was *necessary* to make the P1 gap exceed the margin of error; without it, the measured Kosovo P1 (inflated by international administration) sat close enough to Serbia's that Mod 4 would have demanded deferring to the capacity gate.

**8. VERSION COMPARISON:** LIVE **improved** the defensibility of the call (Mod 1 + Mod 4 interaction) without changing the ordinal outcome. Net: small improvement.

### CASE 10: INDONESIA / EAST TIMOR (TIMOR-LESTE)

**1. Pre-event raw indicators (1998/1999)**

| Indicator | Indonesia 1998/99 | East Timor 1999–2002 |
|---|---|---|
| Macro shock | GDP contracted **13.1%** in 1998; rupiah collapsed ~2,400→14,000/USD | economy destroyed; 80% of infrastructure razed in 1999 |
| Governance | Suharto fell May 1998; chaotic transition | under UNTAET 1999–2002 (UN administration) |
| Oil rents % GDP | ~9.9% (1998), fell to ~0.8% by 2021 | extreme — petroleum later ~70–80% of GDP; IMF: "most oil-dependent economy in the world" |
| P1 of the pair | higher (functioning, if crisis-hit, state) | near-zero indigenous; built from scratch |

**2. Scores (both versions)**
- STATIC: Indonesia higher P1 than East Timor despite the crisis. 
- LIVE: same ordinal P1 call; **Mod 2** triggered for Timor-Leste — petroleum rents far exceed 30% of GDP, so resilience predictions are **inverted** (apparent P4 wealth = vulnerability). **Mod 1** applied to East Timor under UNTAET (measured P1 = administration, not indigenous).

**3. Safeguards**
- **A:** East Timor under UNTAET — STATIC flags; LIVE discounts (Mod 1).
- **E:** Timor-Leste — STATIC flags bidirectionally; LIVE (Mod 2) explicitly inverts. The IMF labels it the most oil-dependent economy in the world. Per IMF Country Report No. 25/278 (2025 Article IV Consultation): "The Petroleum Fund's (PF) balance stood at 939 percent of non-oil GDP in 2024... But oil and gas production has ceased and large withdrawals from the PF to finance fiscal deficits, should they continue, would lead to its full depletion by the end of the 2030s." Textbook rentier vulnerability.
- **B:** 2 successors; Timor-Leste passes capacity gate only with international scaffolding.
- **D:** Indonesia as former occupier (24-year occupation 1975–1999); Australia as stabilizing neighbor (INTERFET, led by Maj. Gen. Peter Cosgrove).
- **G:** 24 years of Tier 1 military suppression → 1999 internationally-supervised referendum. The shift is Tier 1 → exit-via-external-administration, **not** a move down the suppression tiers internally.

**4. Predictions — the critical directional-violence test**
- (a) Trajectory: Indonesia > East Timor (both). 
- (b) **Violence:** *STATIC* Prediction 2 says low P1 (East Timor) predicts violent outcome — but it does **not** specify *who* commits violence, and the violence in fact came from **Indonesia (the higher-P1 member of the pair)**: the militia/TNI scorched-earth campaign. Per the UN CAVR "Chega!" report annex (Geoffrey Robinson): "In the course of 1999... at least 1,200 civilians, and perhaps as many as 1,500, were killed"; Human Rights Watch reports "an estimated 1,500 Timorese were killed, more than 250,000 were forcibly driven across the border into West Timor." *LIVE* uses Safeguard D + the directional tag to correctly attribute violence to the departing predatory power.
- (c) Divergence (both). 
- (f) Timor-Leste reversal/oil-curse risk: LIVE flags via Mod 2; STATIC flags weakly via Safeguard E.
- (g) Tier 1 → exit (both, LIVE cleaner).

**5. Post-event data**
Timor-Leste: independence 20 May 2002; stable politically but undiversified; 42% poverty; HDI declined after 2010 as oil reserves drew down; oil production has now ceased with fund depletion looming. Indonesia: democratized rapidly, GDP grew from ~$95bn (1998) to >$1.3tn (2022); decentralized; remains stable.

**6. Scoring**
- (a) CONFIRMED (both). 
- (b) STATIC **PARTIALLY CONFIRMED / FALSIFIED on attribution** — violence occurred, but from the wrong actor relative to the P1 logic; LIVE **CONFIRMED** (Safeguard D + directional tag correctly assign agency). 
- (c) CONFIRMED (both). 
- (f) CONFIRMED, LIVE stronger (Mod 2). 

**7. Case findings**
This is the cleanest demonstration that **"who commits the violence" matters** and that an unmodified P1-violence rule mis-locates agency. The departing power, not the low-capacity fragment, was the violent actor. Mod 2's inversion for Timor-Leste is also vindicated: the oil wealth that looks like P4 strength is the central long-run vulnerability.

**8. VERSION COMPARISON:** LIVE **clearly improved** accuracy. This is the case that most justifies Safeguard D's elevation and the directional violence tag.

**7. Case findings**
Nigeria is the run's hardest case and forces the most important correction: the tempting "oil rents buy stability" story is **quantitatively false** at the Safeguard-E threshold. Nigeria's unity is a **porosity-plus-capacity-gate** phenomenon, not a rentier-stabilization phenomenon. The framework is *not* falsified by Nigeria's persistence, because it predicts turbulence rather than deterministic breakup — but only the LIVE version (Mod 5 capacity gate + Tier 3 porosity classification) states this correctly; a naive STATIC reading risks over-predicting fragmentation.

**8. VERSION COMPARISON:** LIVE **improved** accuracy and prevented a STATIC over-prediction of breakup. Mod 2's *non*-triggering is itself a validation of the 30% rule's discriminating power.

## Recommendations
- **Adopt the LIVE framework as the working standard going into Run 4**, since it strictly dominated STATIC across Cases 9–12 (better on three, tied on one, worse on none).
- **Treat Safeguard E as threshold-gated, not directional-by-vibes:** apply the 30%-of-GDP inversion mechanically. Nigeria (≤22% total rents, falling to ~11%) is *out*; Timor-Leste (~70–80%) is *in*. Re-audit any prior run that assumed oil "buys stability" without checking the rents-to-GDP ratio.
- **Re-specify the violence prediction** to separate *violence risk* (low P1) from *violence agency* (Safeguard D / departing or predatory power). Score Case 10 STATIC as a partial falsification on attribution in the master log.
- **Promote "re-suppression after porosity" from heuristic to a tested core prediction** given the Case 11 confirmation; flag any state currently dismantling a long-standing porosity/federal-accommodation system as elevated-risk.
- **Benchmarks that would change these recommendations:** if a future high-growth + floor-P1 state (Mod 7) does fragment on schedule, re-promote Mod 7; if a >30%-rent state proves durably stable without inversion signs, soften Mod 2; if a near-margin P1 gap reliably predicts divergent outcomes, relax Mod 4's gate.

## Caveats
- **No precise MI point scores** are reported for Serbia, Kosovo, Indonesia, Timor-Leste, Ethiopia, or Nigeria's non-snapshot years, because the proprietary normalization constants are not publicly recoverable; all calls are ordinal/directional, consistent with the framework's own claims.
- **Data gaps reported, not interpolated:** Nigeria Rule of Law estimates for 2004/2012/2018 were not cleanly retrieved; Kosovo is excluded from FSI entirely; Kosovo and East Timor pre-independence WGI reflect international administration (Safeguard A), not indigenous capacity.
- **WGI vintage discrepancies** (2025 revision vs earlier) are material for Nigeria Political Stability and Control of Corruption extremes; ordinal conclusions are robust but exact values should be treated as approximate.
- **Casualty figures are estimates with wide ranges** (Tigray 300,000–600,000; Biafra 1–3 million; East Timor at least 1,200 and perhaps as many as 1,500 per the UN CAVR "Chega!" report), reported as ranges from named sources (Ghent/Nyssen, AU/Obasanjo, UN CAVR, HRW) rather than point facts. The figure of ≥7,700 conflict deaths in Amhara (April 2023–April 2025) could not be matched to a single authoritative primary source and should be treated as indicative only.
- **Confirmation of a directional prediction is not validation of timing or trigger** — the framework explicitly disclaims both (Mod 3); none of the "CONFIRMED" scores should be read as the framework having predicted *when* or *why* a conflict began.
- This is a **validation exercise on a bespoke framework**, not an established academic index; conclusions about the framework's safeguards are internal to that exercise.
